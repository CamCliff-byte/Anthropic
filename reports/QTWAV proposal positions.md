Capyble should go to QTWAV with firm positions on the guardrails, and leave the content and people choices open to develop together. The guardrails are consent and safety for people on camera, legal accuracy, money, IP and data. **Propose now:** two short modules (about 25–30 minutes for boards, 35–45 minutes for executives and managers) built from 5–7 minute sections; a plain certificate of completion with no accreditation claims; a tiered, opt-in consent and visibility process; the same $250 for everyone on camera, with QC rather than QTWAV's own members approving payments to members; illustrated scenarios voiced by queer and trans actors, with real faces kept for the peer interviews; copyright assigned to QC to hold for QTWAV; anonymous, aggregate uptake data owned by QTWAV; and an external legal review plus a practice review. **Leave open:** who goes on camera, the final outcome wording, the promo plan and the choice of open licence. **Settled:** Billie has confirmed the $40,000 **includes GST**, so plan to a hard cap of $40,000 inc GST ($36,364 ex GST). They are whether money can still be spent after 30 June 2027, and who will host the modules long term. The biggest risks are timing and law. Queensland's standard funding terms deal with "unspent" money and never mention "committed" money, and the last 25% payment (about $9,856 at current settings) falls after 30 June 2027. ## Ten open questions, each with a position ready to say

### 1. Learning outcomes and length: two short modules made of 5–7 minute sections

**Proposed position:** "We propose two short modules. The board module takes about 25–30 minutes. The executive and manager module takes about 35–45 minutes. Each is made of 5–7 minute sections that people can finish over two sittings. Each board section can also run as a 10-minute agenda item. We have drafted three outcomes per module to refine with you at the December workshop."

**Why.** *Evidence:* In 6.9 million video sessions, median engagement topped out at about 6 minutes. Viewers watched nearly all of videos under 6 minutes, and only about 20% of videos 12–40 minutes long ([Guo et al. 2014](https://up.csail.mit.edu/other-pubs/las2014-pguo-engagement.pdf)). That study covered online courses, not board members. A meta-analysis of 56 studies found that splitting material into learner-paced segments gave **small-to-medium gains in retention and transfer**. Learners with more prior knowledge gained more on retention, which suits experienced directors ([Rey et al. 2019](https://www.doi.org/10.1007/S10648-018-9456-4)). A meta-analysis of 260 diversity-training samples found effects were larger when training **combined awareness with skills and sat alongside wider organisational action**. Attitude effects faded over time and knowledge effects lasted ([Bezrukova et al. 2016](https://psycnet.apa.org/doi/10.1037/bul0000067)). *Guidance:* The AHRC warns that "a single educational campaign or a one-off training session is unlikely to result in long-term change". This supports QTWAV's view that the modules complement face-to-face training rather than replace it ([AHRC](https://humanrights.gov.au/sites/default/files/factsheet_-_effective_education_and_training_0_0_0.pdf), quote not checked at source). NFP directors already give a median of **3 days a month** to a single board ([AICD NFP Study 2025–26](https://www.aicd.com.au/content/dam/aicd/pdf/news-media/research/2026/nfp-performance-study-2025-26.pdf), to verify). WorkUP Queensland's governance toolkit for gendered-violence services is built to be used "in chunks", for example in board induction ([WorkUP Qld](https://workup.org.au/resources/feminist-governance-toolkit/)).

*Inference, draft outcomes.* These are modelled on the AHRC standards and the AICD's "questions directors should ask management" approach ([AICD director's guide](https://www.aicd.com.au/organisational-culture/business-ethics/change/a-directors-guide-to-the-positive-duty-to-prevent-workplace-sexual-harassment.html)):

| Module | After completing, a learner can… |
|---|---|
| 1 – Boards | (1) Identify at least three places in governing documents (objects, eligibility criteria, funding contracts, policies) where LGBTIQ+ people may be excluded, and explain why each is a governance risk. (2) Ask at least five specific questions of the CEO about queer and trans staff and clients, and tell a strong answer from a weak one. (3) Describe what visible board leadership on values looks like, and name one action the board could take within 12 months. |
| 2 – Executives and managers | (1) Identify psychosocial hazards that are greater for queer and trans staff in DFSV work, and apply identify–assess–control–review to one scenario. (2) Choose and justify a response to a disclosure that prevents victimisation and does not out anyone. (3) Explain the strengths-based case for recruiting and keeping queer and trans workers, and name two changes to their own practice. |

Two wording rules follow from QTWAV's "not legal advice" condition and from the legal position in section 8. Say "know when to seek legal advice", not "comply with the law". Anchor Module 2's positive-duty content in the federal Sex Discrimination Act, not in Queensland's paused duty. *Inference:* completion will depend more on how QTWAV distributes the modules than on their length. A chair asking the board to finish before a meeting, use in induction, or use as pre-work for face-to-face sessions will all help. If Capyble cites its 70–80% completion rate on the DAF module, it should say whether completion was required. Otherwise the figure looks high next to voluntary benchmarks.

**Status: Propose to QTWAV** for length and structure. Offer the outcomes as drafts to finalise together.

### 2. Certificate and CPD: a plain certificate, no accreditation claims

**Proposed position:** "Each module ends with a certificate of completion. It shows the learner's name, the date, the module, the time taken (about 30 or 40 minutes) and the three outcomes, and is issued in QTWAV's and QC's names. We won't seek formal CPD accreditation. The certificate notes that AICD members can record it as self-directed learning."

**Why.** *Guidance (checked directly):* AICD Members, Graduates and Fellows must earn **20 CPD points a year**. They can self-record learning from other providers and are told to keep certificates, because the AICD audits every month ([AICD CPD](https://www.aicd.com.au/membership/continuing-professional-development.html)). Accredited short courses are much longer. The Governance Institute's NFP fundamentals course runs about 3.5 hours for 6 CPD hours ([Governance Institute](https://www.governanceinstitute.com.au/short-courses/not-for-profit-governance-fundamentals/)). The AASW accepts relevant activities from external providers, which matters because many DFSV managers are social workers ([AASW](https://www.aasw.asn.au/professional/cpd/cpd-overview/)). Rainbow Tick Standard 2 expects organisations to identify LGBTIQ-inclusion training needs for the **governing body** and to keep a training register ([Rainbow Tick Guide](https://rainbowhealthaustralia.org.au/media/pages/rainbow-tick/2565067543-1709686053/rainbow-tick-guide-to-lgbti-inclusive-practice-web.pdf); its 80% figure is unverified). *Inference:* formal accreditation is not realistic on $40,000 for modules this short. A certificate costs little and gives boards evidence for registers and audits. Do not use AICD branding or claim a set number of points. One practical tension: a certificate needs a name, but section 6 recommends anonymous data. Use a certificate that the learner fills in and that is not stored centrally. Capyble still needs to confirm how current Rise 360 handles this.

**Status: Propose to QTWAV.**

### 3. On camera: the people choose their visibility, and consent is tiered

**Proposed position:** "Everyone on camera is a sector peer talking about practice, not about being a victim-survivor. Each person chooses how visible they are, from fully named to voice only. They can move to a less visible level at any time before release. Consent is tiered. Use in these two modules is the base. Wider QTWAV training use, public social clips and anything else are separate opt-ins. Each person previews their own segment. We won't hand over unobscured footage of anyone who chose to be obscured."

**Why.** *Evidence (law):* no Australian law gives contributors a general right to withdraw, approve edits or limit reuse. Those rights exist only if the release creates them. Identifiable footage is personal information, and sexual orientation is "sensitive information" under the Privacy Act ([Privacy Act 1988](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03712/latest/text)). Whether gender identity was added in 2024 is unverified. Small businesses are mostly exempt from the Privacy Act. The new **statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy, in force since 10 June 2025, applies regardless of turnover** ([POLA Act 2024](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2024A00128/latest/text)). *Guidance:* Our Watch says to let people "determine if and how they would like to be identified" and to offer a review of how they are quoted ([Our Watch](https://www.ourwatch.org.au/media-reporting/resources/interviewing-survivors)). Its trans reporting guide, developed with ACON, Transcend and others, warns that people "may be easy to identify … particularly if they come from small communities". It says to plan for backlash, fund support and check in after publication ([Our Watch trans guide](https://assets.ourwatch.org.au/assets/Media-resources/OW_Guidelines_reporting_VA_trans_gender_communities_WEB.pdf)). The ABC's 2025 privacy note says pixelating a face "is not necessarily sufficient to de-identify" someone. It also says consent to an interview is not consent to other uses ([ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/edpols/privacy/105747778)). *Evidence (context):* in December 2025 Queensland extended its pause on new youth puberty-blocker and hormone treatment until 2031 ([Qld Government](https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/104227)). LGBTIQ+ people face online hate at about twice the national rate, 30% against 14% ([eSafety](https://www.esafety.gov.au/lgbtiq/learning-lounge/dealing-with-online-abuse/online-hate-discrimination), secondary).

*Inference, the safeguards to offer.* There are four visibility levels: fully named; first name and generic role; obscured with a pseudonym; voice or text only. Trans and gender-diverse staff, and anyone describing discrimination by an employer people could identify, default to the obscured or voice-only levels unless they actively choose more. Bake the obscuring into the final file and destroy the raw rushes. If an unobscured master sits in a library, the de-identification means nothing. This is the most important practical safeguard. The process has five steps: a paid pre-brief; a "pause" word on the day; questions that steer away from client stories and named employers; a debrief with a support card (QLife 1800 184 527, 1800RESPECT 1800 737 732, DVConnect); and check-ins at 48 hours, at release and two weeks later. Withdrawal: anytime before publication, with the footage destroyed. After publication, the clip comes out at the next scheduled update. No source sets these time limits; they are Capyble's choice. Transcription uses only offline AI tools. No footage goes to cloud AI, model training or voice cloning, and the consent form says so.

*The point to discuss.* QTWAV wants to reuse footage "as they see fit". This is the main conflict with the guidance above. Tier 2 lets QTWAV (through QC) use clips in any non-commercial training, education or advocacy with notice to the contributor. That likely covers what QTWAV means, without the risk to contributors. Public social clips and anything else need separate consent. *Who* goes on camera is QTWAV's call. It knows the peers. Capyble can advise on a mix of board members, executives, managers and workers across 12–16 interviews.

**Status: Propose to QTWAV** for the safeguards. **Discuss with QTWAV** for reuse scope. **Leave open to develop together** for who is filmed.

### 4. Honoraria: the same $250 for everyone, with QC approving payments to members

**Proposed position:** "Everyone who goes on camera is offered the same $250, including QTWAV members. It covers a pre-brief, up to two hours of filming and review of their segment. Travel is reimbursed separately. People can accept it, redirect it to their organisation or a cause, or decline it. QTWAV members help shape this project, so QC, not the QTWAV committee, approves payments to members under its conflict of interest policy."

**Why.** *Guidance:* the Queensland Mental Health Commission's interim rates (February 2026) are **$100 an hour for co-design and co-production** and **$234 per activity of 4 hours or less for guest speakers** ([QMHC](https://www.qmhc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/interim_qmhc_paid_participation_rates.pdf)). Health Consumers Queensland's 2025 statement sets the same $100 an hour. It notes payments may be taxable and may affect Centrelink, and says arrangements should be explained before participation starts ([HCQ](https://www.hcq.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HCQ-Consumer-Payments-Position-Statement-Version-SEPT-2025-LATEST.pdf)). Safe and Equal's 2020 framework says experts by experience should be paid for their time and offered a choice of payment method ([Safe and Equal](https://safeandequal.org.au/wp-content/uploads/DVV_EBE-Framework-Report.pdf)). *Inference:* for 2–3 hours of total effort, $250 works out at $83–$125 an hour, in line with these benchmarks. Sixteen people at $250 is the $4,000 cap.

On tax, Justice Connect's September 2026 guide says "the way a payment is described does not determine its taxation treatment". It says vouchers count as payments, and Centrelink's definition of income is broader than tax law's ([Justice Connect](https://content.nfplaw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Payments-to-consumer-representatives.pdf)). Where a payee gives no ABN and no Statement by a supplier, the payer may have to withhold 47% from payments over $75 ([ATO](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/hiring-and-paying-your-workers/payg-withholding/payments-you-need-to-withhold-from/withholding-from-suppliers/withholding-if-abn-not-provided), secondary). So Capyble should collect an ABN or a Statement by a supplier from every payee. Pay by bank transfer by default. Give a gift card only on request, and say it still counts as payment. On conflicts, ACNC guidance is that conflicted people declare their interest and stay out of the decision ([ACNC](https://www.acnc.gov.au/tools/factsheets/small-charities-library-managing-conflicts-interest), secondary). Paying through Capyble makes the administration simpler, but the conflict is still there. A uniform published rate, QC approval and a note in the auspice paperwork deal with it openly.

*Inference, Billie's case:* the notes do not cover it directly. Billie is QTWAV's one funded worker. If Billie appears on camera during paid hours, an honorarium from the same project budget would pay them twice. Suggest no honorarium for Billie, and offer the same choice to other people filmed in paid work time.

**Status: Propose to QTWAV** for the rule and QC approval. **Discuss with QTWAV** for Billie and for people filmed in work time.

### 5. Scenarios and video: illustrate the dilemmas, film the people

**Proposed position:** "Scenarios will be illustrated, voiced by queer and trans actors, with branching choices. Real faces come from the filmed peer interviews. From the interviews we cut 2–4 minute topic videos to use inside the modules and in your face-to-face sessions. We also cut short promos and social clips from the same footage. A filmed, acted vignette is available as a priced extra."

**Why.** *Evidence:* across 24 studies, high-fidelity simulation showed **no significant advantage** over low-fidelity, with average differences of 1–2% ([Norman et al. 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/j.1365-2923.2012.04243.x)). In a review of animation in health education, 8 of 10 knowledge studies favoured animation, although the number of trials was small ([Springer 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40037-022-00736-6)). Human-like gesture and voice help learning, and realistic faces add little by themselves ([Mayer](https://www.unh.edu/teaching-learning-resource-hub/sites/default/files/media/2023-06/itow-research-based-principles-for-designing-multimedia-instruction-mayer.pdf)). Real people matter most where contact and story do the work. Ten-minute conversations reduced anti-trans prejudice for at least three months ([Broockman & Kalla 2016](https://www.ocf.berkeley.edu/~broockma/broockman_kalla_transphobia_canvassing_experiment.pdf)), and a later video-narrative condition also worked ([Kalla & Broockman](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/reducing-exclusionary-attitudes-through-interpersonal-conversation-evidence-from-three-field-experiments/4AA5B97806A4CAFBAB0651F5DAD8F223/share/e0daeef3010c1ee77a7b10d603eafac028bd40a7)). A poor portrayal can backfire ([Communication Studies 2021](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/10510974.2021.1876125)). *Guidance:* GLAAD says to cast trans actors in trans roles and to correct misgendering on screen ([GLAAD](https://assets.glaad.org/m/6b239e91c8c5d18c/original/Guide-for-LGBTQ-Inclusion-in-Entertainment-English.pdf)). Australian vendors quote about **A$1,200 a finished minute for template animation** and **A$2,000–8,000 for a simple live-action training video**, rising to A$10,000+ with several actors ([instructionaldesign.com.au](https://instructionaldesign.com.au/how-much-does-elearning-development-cost/); [Visual Domain](https://www.visualdomain.com.au/blog/how-much-does-training-video-production-cost-in-australia)). These are vendor figures, not independent benchmarks. *Inference:* a filmed branching scenario needs a new shot for every branch, which would use up the budget. Illustration also keeps workers anonymous in a small sector, and scripts can be changed cheaply if the law moves after March 2027. No study shows illustration is emotionally safer, so don't claim it.

On promos, *guidance:* LinkedIn recommends **15–30 second** videos with subtitles and lists 4:5 as its preferred vertical ratio ([LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/a424737)). It claims about 80% of users watch with the sound off ([LinkedIn](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/ads/sponsored-content/video-ads/tips), platform self-report). *No evidence was found that trailers raise course enrolment.* *Inference:* make a 30–45 second master with 15 second and 6–10 second cut-downs, and burn in the captions. Name the audience in the first seconds. Judge promos on click-throughs and completions, measured with tagged links, not on views. Social clips can only use people who opted in to public use. Module 1 goes live on 11 June 2027, so IDAHOBIT (17 May) and Queensland's DV Prevention Month (May) suit "coming soon" teasers. Wear It Purple (late August) falls after both modules are live. Directors are more likely to be reached through intermediaries such as WorkUP, peaks and QC newsletters, and through a board-pack insert with a QR code, than through organic social media.

*Accessibility:* Articulate's September 2026 conformance report says Rise 360 output supports every WCAG 2.2 A and AA criterion except 3.1.2, Language of Parts ([Articulate VPAT](https://www.articulate.com/about/accessibility/rise-360-accessibility-conformance-report-vpat/)). This is a vendor self-report. It is newer than Articulate's earlier "broadly supports WCAG 2.1 AA" wording, which the learning-design notes relied on. Capyble still has to supply edited captions, transcripts, scene descriptions and a described version of any video that carries visual-only information.

**Status: Propose to QTWAV** for the format. **Leave open to develop together** for the promo plan and release calendar, because they run on QTWAV's channels and its peers' networks.

### 6. IP, licence, hosting and data: QC holds it, QTWAV can run it without Capyble

**Proposed position:** "The contract is with QC as auspice for QTWAV. Capyble assigns copyright in everything made for the project to QC on payment, to hold for QTWAV's benefit. Capyble keeps its own templates and methods but licenses them permanently for use in the modules. From day one the public link sits on a web address QTWAV owns. By the end of the grant QTWAV holds everything it needs to keep the modules running without us. QTWAV owns the uptake data, which is anonymous and aggregate."

**Why: ownership.** *Evidence:* an unincorporated association "can't enter into contracts in its own name", and it may be unclear who owns its property. The auspice agreement should say who owns IP during the arrangement and after it ends ([Justice Connect auspicing guide](https://content.nfplaw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Auspicing-guide.pdf)). QC's legal name is Queensland Council for LGBTI Health, ABN 58 039 823 994. QTWAV is not one of its registered business names ([ABN Lookup](https://abr.business.gov.au/ABN/View/58039823994)). Under Queensland's standard funding terms (v1.2, July 2025), IP in material made "by You or on Your behalf" vests in the funded body (cl 16.1). That body must give the State a **perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free, sub-licensable licence** (cl 16.2). Subcontracting needs the department's **prior written consent** (cl 22.1). The funding must be acknowledged with a departmental logo (cl 10) ([Standard Terms](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)). These are the standard terms; QC's actual agreement has not been seen. *Inference:* Capyble's licence of its background IP must allow sub-licensing to the State. Stock music and footage must allow it too. Capyble needs a licence-back for portfolio use. QC should say what happens to the IP if QTWAV incorporates, winds up or changes auspice.

**Why: licence.** *Guidance:* Queensland Government policy makes **CC BY 4.0 the default** for State-owned information ([QGEA](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/information-technology/queensland-government-enterprise-architecture-qgea/qgea-directions-and-guidance/qgea-policies-standards-and-guidelines/open-data-information-sharing-access-and-use-policy)). ANROWS publishes under CC BY-NC 4.0 ([ANROWS](https://www.anrows.org.au/publication/rante-rante-ampe-marle-and-urreye-safe-respected-and-free-from-violence-projects-evaluation/read-summary/)). Our Watch keeps its copyright ([Our Watch](https://www.ourwatch.org.au/copyright)). *Inference:* suggest CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 for the finished modules. That keeps them free for the sector and stops a private trainer from reselling them. CC BY suits QTWAV better if maximum reuse is the goal. Never openly license raw footage. Creative Commons licences cannot be revoked, and contributors may later need their clips taken down for safety.

**Why: hosting.** *Guidance (vendor):* Quick Share gives a public link with no login. It comes with Articulate 360, and since October 2026 it has an analytics dashboard (sessions, completions, time) and a survey block ([Articulate FAQ](https://www.articulate.com/faq/); [What's New](https://www.articulate.com/360/whats-new/)). Reach Starter allows 300 active learners a year, needs logins and comes only with Teams seats ([Articulate pricing](https://www.articulate.com/360/pricing/)). Connected Packages is still a beta ("Labs") feature. Its content is served from the author's Articulate account, so client copies break if the source course is deleted ([MicroWay](https://www.microway.com.au/blog/articulate-connected-packages-new-lab-feature/)). Users have also reported resume errors after republishing ([E-Learning Heroes](https://community.articulate.com/discussions/discuss/rise-360-connected-package-not-working-for-learners-after-republishing/1268177)). When a subscription ends, cloud services stop, but exported courses keep running ([Articulate FAQ](https://www.articulate.com/faq/)). Old forum replies say Quick Share links die when the licence lapses ([forum](https://community.articulate.com/discussions/rise-360/does-a-rise-preview-link-ever-expire)). SCORM Cloud's "Little" plan costs **US$90 a month (about A$1,515 a year)** for 50 registrations a month. It is US-hosted, and overage charges can be switched off ([Rustici](https://rusticisoftware.com/products/scorm-cloud/pricing/)). *Inference:* for the two-year phase, use Quick Share with the guestbook off and the survey on, plus standard SCORM 1.2 files for organisations with an LMS. Don't promise Connected Packages to a funder. Before October 2027, hand over web and SCORM exports, video masters, captions, transcripts and a register of third-party asset licences. Point a QTWAV-owned short address at the course so the link never changes when hosting moves. After two years the permanent copy is a web export on a QTWAV, QC or partner host. Ask QC and WorkUP whether they can host it. If QTWAV wants one dataset covering public and LMS learners, SCORM Cloud roughly matches the $1,500-a-year hosting line. Paying for hosting within the grant period also keeps that money "spent" (see section 7).

**Why: data.** *Guidance:* the Queensland Privacy Principles have applied since 1 July 2025. Agencies must bind contracted providers, including to the overseas-disclosure rule ([OIC](https://www.oic.qld.gov.au/government/privacy/contractors-privacy)). Properly de-identified aggregate data falls outside the principles, but small groups raise the risk of re-identification ([OIC](https://www.oic.qld.gov.au/guidelines/for-government/guidelines-privacy-principles/use-and-disclosure/data-analytics)). *Inference:* collect starts, completions, time spent, self-reported organisation type, role and region, and a pre/post confidence rating. Never ask learners about their own sexuality or gender. Suppress any count under 5. QTWAV owns the data and gets a quarterly report. Capyble uses aggregates in its own reporting only with QTWAV's permission.

**Status: Propose to QTWAV** for ownership and data. **Discuss with QTWAV** for the licence choice, which is QTWAV's decision. **Discuss with QTWAV** for long-term hosting, which depends on who can host.

### 7. Money and sign-off: work to $40,000 inc GST, protect the last payment, one person signs off

**Proposed position:** "Our quote comes in at or under $40,000 including GST. Each line shows the ex-GST amount, the GST and the total. We invoice QC and reference the QTWAV project. Payments are 50% at kick-off, 25% at the module 1 draft and 25% at final delivery. If QC needs everything paid by 30 June 2027, we'll move the last milestone earlier. QC signs the contract. ## Conclusion

The proposal's real value to QTWAV is not features. It is a set of modules that keep working after the funded role ends. In practice that means three things. Content can be edited cheaply when the law changes, which is why illustration and a "law as at" date matter. Footage cannot later harm the people in it, which is why obscuring is baked in, raw files are destroyed and raw footage is never openly licensed. And QTWAV owns the files, the address and the data outright. Each position Capyble proposes serves one of these three aims, so Capyble can explain the proposal as one coherent offer rather than a list of terms.

The project's pressure point is April to June 2027. In that window the Flanagan report lands (31 March), the legal review has to happen, the Module 1 draft is due (14 May), Module 1 goes live (11 June), and the 30 June question falls. If any of these slips, the others are affected. Capyble should plan buffer into that window and settle the spend-by question with QC before the contract is signed. It is hard to change once the contract is in place. The price cap is settled, so the remaining money risk is timing, not amount.
