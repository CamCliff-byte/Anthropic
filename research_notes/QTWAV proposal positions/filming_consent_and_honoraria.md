## 1. Consent for filmed lived/practice-experience content: informed and ongoing consent, scope of reuse, withdrawal, review of edits, release wording when the client reuses footage

### Takeaway
No Australian law gives contributors a general right to withdraw, approve edits or limit reuse. Those rights exist only if the release or consent form creates them. DFV-sector and broadcaster guidance (Our Watch, ABC, Safe and Equal) is consistent on what the form should do: say exactly what each use is, let people choose how they are identified, offer a review of how they're quoted or shown, take withdrawal requests seriously, and never treat consent to an interview as consent to other uses. QTWAV's plan to reuse the footage "as they see fit" is the main conflict with that guidance. The fix is tiered, opt-in consent with a named custodian and a review date.

### Cited Findings
**DFV-sector media guidance (good practice, not law)**
- Our Watch "Interviewing survivors" says to be aware of the "inherent power imbalance between media organisations and survivors" (it can make consent less voluntary), to "Ensure you have informed consent to disclose elements of their story", to "Let them determine if and how they would like to be identified" (anonymous, pseudonym, voice actor, blurred images), to "Respect their agency if they do choose to be identified", to let them choose the location and bring a support person, and to "Offer the opportunity to review the way they are referred to or quoted". The page shows no publication date (footer © 2026) and doesn't explicitly cover withdrawal after consent. — [Our Watch, Interviewing survivors](https://www.ourwatch.org.au/media-reporting/resources/interviewing-survivors)
- Our Watch image guidelines recommend ongoing consent where images stay in use for a long time, and giving survivors the chance to view images before they air. Our Watch sexual-harassment reporting tips say survivors can withdraw consent at any time without pressure, blame or shame (via Perplexity; not opened directly). — [Our Watch image guidelines](https://www.ourwatch.org.au/media-reporting/resources/image-guidelines-for-media-reporting-of-violence-against-women); [Our Watch, 12 tips for reporting on sexual harassment](https://www.ourwatch.org.au/media-reporting/resources/12-tips-for-reporting-on-sexual-harassment)
- The Our Watch trans reporting guide (developed with Transgender Victoria, Trans Justice Project, Zoe Belle Gender Collective, Black Rainbow, ACON, Rainbow Health Australia and Transcend) says: "Be clear about the scope and intention of the story, including what you hope to achieve, and any risks they may face"; ask "what is on and off the record? Are any areas of discussion off limits?"; "If possible, share your story with sources and case studies in advance of publication"; and make sure sources know that "anything shared publicly may become available on search engines or social media, be syndicated to other outlets, or be mis/quoted in other contexts." — [Our Watch, Guide for media reporting on violence against trans and gender diverse communities (PDF)](https://assets.ourwatch.org.au/assets/Media-resources/OW_Guidelines_reporting_VA_trans_gender_communities_WEB.pdf). **Dating note:** PDF metadata shows creation on 9 April 2024. Perplexity said "4 November 2025" and its webpage is described as updated October 2026. Treat it as 2024 guidance that is still current. [Webpage](https://www.ourwatch.org.au/media-reporting/resources/reporting-violence-against-transgender-gender-diverse-communities)
- The Safe and Equal (then DV Vic) Family Violence Experts by Experience Framework (2020) says survivor advocates should get clarity about how they will be remunerated, tenure, time commitments and scope of their role, and be "financially remunerated for their time, contributions and expenses when they provide significant input into policy and practice". **This is 2020 Victorian guidance.** — [Safe and Equal, EBE Framework 2020 (PDF)](https://safeandequal.org.au/wp-content/uploads/DVV_EBE-Framework-Report.pdf)

**Broadcaster standards (editorial standards, not law)**
- ABC Editorial Guidance Note on privacy (issued 8 September 2025): "the absence of an objection will not automatically be taken to be consent"; "Consent should not be obtained by deception"; "Consent to an interview does not necessarily amount to consent to use additional personal information"; "Careful consideration should be given to any reasonable objections or requests to withdraw consent." — [ABC Edpols, Privacy guidance note](https://www.abc.net.au/edpols/privacy/105747778)
- ABC standards require it to honour assurances given to participants about conditions of participation, use of content, confidentiality or anonymity, except in rare public-interest cases (via Perplexity; not opened directly). — [ABC Edpols, 5. Fair and honest dealing](https://www.abc.net.au/edpols/5-fair-and-honest-dealing/13635406)
- I found no primary Screen Australia, Screen Producers Australia, SBS or Documentary Australia text on contributor withdrawal or edit review. Standard talent releases grant broad all-media, perpetual, sublicensable rights (via Perplexity, general industry description; no primary source).

**Law (Australian Privacy Principles and related)**
- Identifiable footage is "personal information". "Sensitive information" under s 6(1) of the Privacy Act 1988 includes sexual orientation and gender identity (the latter added by the 2024 amendments, *to verify*). Under APP 3.3, an APP entity generally needs consent to collect sensitive information. — [Privacy Act 1988 (Cth)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03712/latest/text)
- Small business exemption (s 6D): businesses with turnover of $3M or less are generally not APP entities. Exceptions include providing a health service, holding health information, disclosing personal information for a benefit, and Commonwealth contracted service providers. — [Privacy Act s 6D](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03712/latest/text)
- The *Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024* created a statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy (Sch 2, commenced 10 June 2025). It is **not** limited to APP entities, so it applies to small businesses and unincorporated groups. — [POLA Act 2024](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2024A00128/latest/text)
- The Qld *Information Privacy Act 2009* (QPPs from 1 July 2025) mainly binds Queensland public-sector agencies. A private contractor is bound only through a service arrangement with an agency. — [Information Privacy Act 2009 (Qld)](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-2009-014)

**Copyright**
- Copyright in a film or sound recording is separate from any copyright in the speaker's words. Moral rights (attribution, integrity) apply to authors of works and to films. A release should cover both the recording and the contributor's words and supplied material (via Perplexity; general summary of the [Copyright Act 1968](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1968A00063/latest)).

### Inferences
- **Good practice (strongly supported)**: treat consent as a process with written checkpoints: (1) plain-language information sheet at invitation; (2) signed consent before filming; (3) verbal re-confirmation on camera; (4) review of the person's edited segment(s) and how they are named/captioned before publication; (5) re-consent before any new use outside the agreed scope.
- **Proposed position on reuse ("as they see fit")**: replace open-ended reuse with **tiered opt-in consent**. Each contributor ticks separately:
  - Tier 1: use in the two Capyble/QTWAV modules (named, described).
  - Tier 2: use by QTWAV (through its auspice, QC) in other **non-commercial** training, education and advocacy about safe, inclusive DFSV workplaces, with no new consent needed but a notice to the contributor.
  - Tier 3: short public clips on social media or websites (higher abuse risk; separate opt-in).
  - Tier 4: anything else (media, third parties, commercial licensing, AI training) needs fresh consent.
- **Re-editing for new contexts**: the release should say QTWAV may not edit clips in a way that changes their meaning, and may not put a contributor's words next to content they would reasonably object to. This reflects the moral-rights integrity concept.
- **Withdrawal**: propose (a) withdrawal at any time before publication, no reason needed, with footage destroyed; (b) after publication, the clip is removed from the modules at the next scheduled update (e.g. within 30 days) and removed from the QTWAV library, with no new uses; (c) a plain statement that already-downloaded or shared copies can't be recalled. Our Watch and ABC support honouring withdrawal requests. Time limits like these are the producer's choice. No source mandates a figure.
- **Review of edits**: offer each person a preview of their own segment, with a defined right to require changes for **accuracy, safety or identification**. Editorial control of the module as a whole stays with Capyble/QTWAV. This follows the distinction in ABC and Our Watch practice and is defensible for pre-recorded training.
- **Time limit / review date**: no source sets one. Suggest consent is valid for the module's life or 3 years, whichever is shorter, after which the custodian re-confirms (an annual check is a stronger option). Inference only.
- **Who holds consent obligations after handover**: QTWAV is unincorporated, so the library custodian is in practice QC as auspice. QC probably provides health services, so it may be an APP entity whatever its turnover. **Check with QC whether it is covered by the Privacy Act.** If it is, footage that reveals gender identity or sexual orientation is sensitive information, and reuse outside the original purpose generally needs consent (APP 6). The consent register and copies of signed releases should go to QC with the footage.
- **Capyble's own position**: Capyble is probably under the $3M threshold and may not be an APP entity. The statutory privacy tort still applies, so APP-standard handling is the prudent baseline. **Seek legal advice on the release wording**, especially copyright ownership and assignment/licence to QC/QTWAV, moral-rights consents, and the tiered reuse clause.
- Sector peers (board members, executives) describing practice should also be warned not to disclose identifiable client, colleague or workplace information. The release should let Capyble cut any such content.

### Gaps
- I couldn't find a Safe and Equal, ANROWS, DVNSW, DVConnect or Mental Health Australia document that sets a specific withdrawal window, edit-review right or reuse rule for filmed stories. Perplexity's comparative table on these bodies was generic and is **not** relied on here.
- No primary Screen Australia, SPA or SBS contributor-release text was found.
- Who owns copyright in a *commissioned* film under the Copyright Act (s 98, commissioning provisions) and in the interviewee's spoken words wasn't verified. Get legal advice before promising QTWAV ownership.
- I didn't verify whether "gender identity" now appears in the s 6(1) sensitive-information definition after POLA 2024. Sexual orientation ("sexual orientation or practices") is long-standing.

## 2. De-identification options for queer and trans participants; the 2025–26 Queensland climate and online abuse risk; safety planning

### Takeaway
Blurring, voice alteration and first names reduce risk but don't guarantee anonymity, especially in a small Queensland DFSV workforce where colleagues can recognise someone from context. In 2025–26 the climate in Queensland became more hostile for trans people: the youth gender-affirming care pause was extended to 2031, reforms to anti-discrimination law are on indefinite hold, and LGBTIQ+ people face disproportionate online hate. Participant-led choice of visibility, layered de-identification and a written pre- and post-publication safety plan are the recommended practice.

### Cited Findings
**De-identification guidance**
- Our Watch lists anonymity, pseudonym, voice actor and blurred images as options. The survivor decides "if and how they would like to be identified." — [Our Watch, Interviewing survivors](https://www.ourwatch.org.au/media-reporting/resources/interviewing-survivors)
- The Our Watch trans guide says: "Allow the use of aliases for safety and be clear with sources in advance when anonymity is not possible"; "Use only the details your source has given regarding full names, pronouns, physical identity, and location. Individuals may use different identity markers publicly than they use in other situations"; "Do not use a trans person's birth name (or 'dead name') without explicit consent"; "Only include a person's transgender status and/or assigned sex at birth if it's relevant"; "'Outing' someone as transgender can have severe consequences for their safety, relationships and employment. Some trans and gender diverse people may be out in certain contexts but may not want to be out publicly"; and survivors "may be easy to identify ... particularly if they come from small communities or rural and regional areas." It also says not to ask about genitals, hormones or surgical history. — [Our Watch trans guide (PDF, 2024)](https://assets.ourwatch.org.au/assets/Media-resources/OW_Guidelines_reporting_VA_trans_gender_communities_WEB.pdf)
- ABC (Sept 2025): "De-identification means making sure we are not identifying the person to anyone who would not already know"; "Pixelation of a person's face or removal of identifying information such as a name ... is not necessarily sufficient to de-identify the person." — [ABC privacy guidance note](https://www.abc.net.au/edpols/privacy/105747778)
- The OAIC says information is de-identified only when the risk of re-identification in context is very low. Removing names or blurring isn't automatically enough. Voice, gait, distinctive features and other available information all count (via Perplexity; not opened directly). — [OAIC, De-identification and the Privacy Act](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/guidance-and-advice/de-identification-and-the-privacy-act)

**Queensland climate 2025–26**
- 19 December 2025: the Qld Government released the Vine review and said it would keep the pause on starting Stage 1 (puberty suppression) and Stage 2 (hormone) treatment for minors in the public system until the UK PATHWAYS trial reports in 2031. Current patients continue treatment. — [Qld Ministerial statement, "Independent Review into puberty blockers released", 19 Dec 2025](https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/104227)
- The pause began with a Health Service Directive on 28 January 2025. — [Qld Health, hormone therapies review](https://www.health.qld.gov.au/research-reports/reports/review-investigation/hormone-therapies-review)
- January 2026: the Minister amended the Directive to define a "new patient" (no care before 28 January 2025), reported as a way to avoid a Supreme Court test. Judicial review and a discrimination/human rights complaint were reported as ongoing. In July 2026 an e-petition sought reinstatement of the care. — [QNews, Directive amendment](https://qnews.com.au/qld-health-minister-quietly-amends-trans-health-directive-avoiding-court/); [QNews, petition (July 2026)](https://qnews.com.au/new-petition-urges-queensland-parliament-to-reinstate-trans-healthcare/) *(community news outlet; not checked against court records)*
- The major provisions of the *Respect at Work and Other Matters Amendment Act 2024* (Qld), originally due 1 July 2025, were deferred in 2025 to a date set by proclamation. These include a positive duty and new attributes and vilification protections. They had not been proclaimed as at October 2026, and the *Anti-Discrimination Act 1991* still applies (via Perplexity, citing Qld legislation register; commencement status not checked directly). — [Respect at Work and Other Matters Amendment Act 2024](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/asmade/act-2024-047); [Qld statement](https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/102168)
- Our Watch trans guide statistics: in a national study, "1 in 2 trans people had experienced anti-trans hate; and, 1 in 10 trans people had experienced anti-trans violence." — [Our Watch trans guide](https://assets.ourwatch.org.au/assets/Media-resources/OW_Guidelines_reporting_VA_trans_gender_communities_WEB.pdf)
- eSafety: LGBTIQ+ people experience online hate speech at more than twice the national rate (30% vs 14%) (via Perplexity; eSafety site returned 503 so this wasn't opened directly). — [eSafety, Online hate & discrimination (LGBTIQ+)](https://www.esafety.gov.au/lgbtiq/learning-lounge/dealing-with-online-abuse/online-hate-discrimination); [eSafety, how the OSA protects those most at risk](https://www.esafety.gov.au/communities/how-online-safety-act-protects-those-most-at-risk)
- eSafety defines doxxing as publishing personal information without consent and recognises gendered doxxing, including publishing a trans person's location to encourage harassment (via Perplexity). — [eSafety, types of online abuse (LGBTIQ+)](https://www.esafety.gov.au/lgbtiq/learning-lounge/dealing-with-online-abuse/types-online-abuse)

**Safety planning (Our Watch trans guide)**
- "Understand that your story may receive backlash. Put backlash mitigation and response strategies in place." "Ensure your source is aware of the risk of backlash and provide advice about how to manage online abuse, including hiding online profiles if necessary." "Share your organisation's policies on managing online backlash." Use filters, "strictly moderat[e] comment sections", "Actively hide or delete harmful online comments. Disable comment sections if unmoderated." "Extend your organisation's Employment Assistance Program to them or fund specialist mental health support"; offer QLife; "Check in with your interviewees post-publication." It also says to encourage sources to connect with a trans organisation for media coaching. — [Our Watch trans guide](https://assets.ourwatch.org.au/assets/Media-resources/OW_Guidelines_reporting_VA_trans_gender_communities_WEB.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Proposed visibility menu** (each participant picks one; any participant can drop down a level up to publication):
  1. **Full identification**: face, name, role, organisation. Suits executives and board members speaking in a professional capacity who are already publicly out or don't discuss their own identity.
  2. **Partial**: face, first name only (or chosen name), generic role, no employer. Warn that in a small Queensland DFSV sector, face plus role is usually identifying.
  3. **Obscured**: silhouette, back-of-head, hands or cutaways, plus a pseudonym and stripped context (no suburb, employer, distinctive story details). Add voice alteration if the voice is distinctive. Tell participants that blur and pitch shifting can sometimes be undone and are no protection against people who already know them.
  4. **Voice-only or text**: audio over illustration or animation, or the words re-voiced by another person or shown as on-screen text. Use this for anyone not out at work, with family-violence, visa or employment risks, or who has had targeted abuse.
- **Recommendation**: default trans and gender-diverse staff, and anyone describing workplace discrimination by a current or recent employer, to levels 3–4 unless they actively choose higher visibility. Executives, managers and board members describing organisational practice will usually be at levels 1–2.
- **Technical**: for obscured contributors, don't keep or hand over an un-obscured master in the QTWAV library. Bake in the obscuring and the voice alteration, and destroy the raw files (see Q5). Otherwise the "library" undoes the de-identification. This is the most important practical safeguard. Agree it with QTWAV.
- Captions, metadata and file names in the library must use the agreed name only (no legal or dead names).
- **Safety plan per participant** (one page): preferred name and pronouns; out/not-out contexts; their visibility level; specific details never to include; social media hygiene checklist (eSafety); who monitors comments where clips are public (modules on an LMS have low comment exposure, social clips high); a single contact for takedown requests; check-ins at release and about 2 weeks later; support referrals. Public social clips (Tier 3) should be opt-in and, if used, comments off or moderated.
- Since the Qld climate is contested and politically charged, assume any public trans-visible clip could be clipped or misused out of context. Explain this in plain language at consent.

### Gaps
- I couldn't verify reported 2025–26 funding cuts or non-renewals for QC, Open Doors or other Queensland LGBTIQ+ services. Search returned only older (2012–13) cuts ([Autostraddle on 2012 QAHC defunding](https://autostraddle.com/defunding-of-queer-health-resources-means-it-kinda-sucks-to-be-gay-in-queensland-right-now-141050)). Don't assert 2025–26 cuts without QC confirming them.
- I found no specific ACON, Equality Australia, Transcend, Minus18 or QC safety-planning guide for media participants. The Our Watch guide was co-developed with ACON, Transcend and others and is the best available proxy. Ask QC whether it has its own community-voice or media safety guidance.
- I found no reliable Queensland-specific data on online abuse of people who appear in public LGBTIQ+ content, and no verified 2025–26 incidents at Queensland LGBTIQ+ events.

## 3. Honoraria: benchmarks, already-paid staff, paying QTWAV's own members, gift cards vs cash, tax/ABN/GST, Centrelink

### Takeaway
$250 for a 1–2 hour filmed interview meets the Queensland benchmarks ($100/hr for co-design/production at QMHC Level 3 and HCQ "Influence"; $234 per activity of 4 hours or less for guest speakers at QMHC Level 4), as long as prep and review time are within about 2.5 hours in total. The harder issues are not the rate. They are: (a) whether to pay people already paid by their employer to attend, (b) the conflict when QTWAV members are paid from QTWAV's own project budget, and (c) tax and Centrelink, because labelling the payment an "honorarium" or using a gift card doesn't change how it's treated.

### Cited Findings
**Rates**
- QMHC "interim minimum rates table – as of February 2026": Level 1 $25/hr; Level 2 $50/hr (one-off consultations, feedback on training materials); **Level 3 $100/hr** (short-term advisory; "Co-design and/or co-production activities and projects"; selection panels); **Level 4 $234 per activity 4 hours and under / $469 over 4 hours** (sitting fees; host community-led discussions; "Guest speaker/panel speaker/master of ceremonies", where payment is appropriate, negotiated and pre-approved). The rates are "interim ... as the Commission is in the process of reviewing our paid participation policy" and "benchmarked to Health Consumers Queensland – Consumer Payments Position Statement – 2025". Travel and expenses follow Qld Government part-time chairs and members remuneration procedures. — [QMHC interim rates (PDF)](https://www.qmhc.qld.gov.au/sites/default/files/interim_qmhc_paid_participation_rates.pdf); [QMHC Paid participation](https://www.qmhc.qld.gov.au/about/funding/paid-participation)
- HCQ Consumer Payments Position Statement 2025: sitting fees $234 per meeting of 4 hours or less and $469 over 4 hours, both including pre-reading and reasonable travel time and indexed annually to CPI. "Influence" (short-term advisory, **co-design**, "design, review and improvement of consumer resources") is **$100/hr** in 15-minute increments. "Collaborate" (one-off advisory, feedback on "brochures and training materials") is $50/hr. For surveys and forums, "a minimum value of $25 (e.g. a gift card or similar) is recommended." It assumes GST is not included and states: "**Sitting fees and gifts are generally not assessable for GST purposes.** There may be GST implications ... where the person is performing a service through a contract." It also says payments "may be taxable income for the health consumer and may also impact their Centrelink or Veterans' Affairs entitlements"; "regular payments for ongoing effort may create an employment relationship"; and payment arrangements should be "explained prior to the beginning of consumer participation" and paid promptly. Where consumers bring specialised professional skills to technical committees, it recommends commercial rates. — [HCQ Position Statement 2025 (PDF)](https://www.hcq.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HCQ-Consumer-Payments-Position-Statement-Version-SEPT-2025-LATEST.pdf)
- Safe and Equal EBE Framework (2020): survivor advocates should be paid for their time, contributions and expenses. The literature suggests "offering experts by experience an option for the method of payment (such as cash or vouchers) was useful for those for whom payment may impact other entitlements." — [EBE Framework 2020](https://safeandequal.org.au/wp-content/uploads/DVV_EBE-Framework-Report.pdf)

**Tax and ABN**
- ATO "Honorariums": an honorarium is "an honorary reward for voluntary services" or "a fee for professional services voluntarily performed". "Honorary rewards for voluntary services are not assessable income"; "fees received for professional services voluntarily provided are assessable income". Example: a council programmer who volunteers as a referee gets $100, not assessable. A graphic designer who prepares a gallery report using her business's equipment gets $800, assessable "because it is a reward for services connected to her income-producing activities" (via Perplexity; ATO returned 403 so the page wasn't opened directly). — [ATO, Honorariums](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/not-for-profit-organisations/your-organisation/your-obligations-to-volunteers-employees-and-contractors/not-for-profit-volunteers/paying-volunteers/honorariums)
- Justice Connect/NFP Law, *Consumer representative payments* (Sep 2026): "The way a payment is described does not determine its taxation treatment." A volunteer payment is less likely to be assessable where the person doesn't rely on it, the organisation isn't obliged to pay, payment isn't the main reason for participating, and the amount is token. But "Where a payment is offered as an incentive to participate and the recipient participates because of that payment, the amount received may be assessable income." A person carrying on a business generally must get an ABN. "Where a consumer representative does not provide an ABN, the organisation may be required to withhold an amount ... unless an exception applies," and "A representative who does not provide an ABN because the activity is a hobby should generally provide a Statement by Supplier." Vouchers are payments too (its example: $50 vouchers for a 3-hour focus group). It does not cover board-member payments (see its separate fact sheet). — [Justice Connect, Consumer representative payments, Sep 2026 (PDF)](https://content.nfplaw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Payments-to-consumer-representatives.pdf); earlier edition [Aug 2024](https://content.nfplaw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Payments-to-consumer-representatives.pdf)
- No-ABN withholding is 47% when a supplier doesn't quote an ABN and the payment for the supply exceeds $75 excluding GST. The Statement by a supplier exception covers hobby or wholly private/domestic supplies (via Perplexity). — [ATO, Withholding if ABN not provided](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/hiring-and-paying-your-workers/payg-withholding/payments-you-need-to-withhold-from/withholding-from-suppliers/withholding-if-abn-not-provided); [ATO, Statement by a supplier not quoting an ABN](https://www.ato.gov.au/forms-and-instructions/statement-by-supplier-not-quoting-an-abn)
- TR 2005/13 (gifts): a payment made to obtain services is not a gift (via Perplexity). — [ATO TR 2005/13](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?DocID=TXR/TR200513/NAT/ATO/00001)

**Centrelink**
- Justice Connect (Sep 2026): "the definition of income for social security purposes is broad and may include ... payments received for a person's benefit ... some gifts, allowances", and "A payment may therefore be relevant for Centrelink purposes even if it is not assessable income for taxation purposes." Organisations may encourage people to seek independent advice. — [Justice Connect 2026](https://content.nfplaw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/Payments-to-consumer-representatives.pdf)
- Services Australia tells recipients to report money from a one-off job, gift or allowance, "no matter how small" (via Perplexity; the site returned 503). — [Services Australia, reporting other types of income](https://www.servicesaustralia.gov.au/employment-income-reporting-if-you-have-other-types-income?context=45541)

**Conflict of interest and paying members**
- ACNC: paying Responsible People isn't prohibited if the governing documents allow it and it's reasonable and properly authorised. Conflicted people must disclose and not take part in the decision. Payments to Responsible People are related-party transactions (via Perplexity). — [ACNC, Remunerating Responsible People](https://www.acnc.gov.au/tools/guides/remunerating-responsible-people); [ACNC, Managing conflicts of interest](https://www.acnc.gov.au/tools/factsheets/small-charities-library-managing-conflicts-interest); [ACNC, Related party transactions](https://www.acnc.gov.au/tools/topic-guides/related-party-transactions)

### Inferences
- **Rate position**: $250 flat for up to 2 hours on camera plus pre-brief and review is defensible against QMHC Level 3 ($100/hr) and Level 4 guest speaker ($234 per activity of 4 hours or less). Including about 30 min pre-brief and about 30 min segment review, the effective rate is about $83–$125/hr. Recommend stating "$250 per participant, covering pre-brief, filming (up to 2 hrs) and review of your segment", plus travel and parking reimbursed separately as HCQ recommends. The $4,000 cap funds 16 participants at $250, which matches the 12–16 interview plan. Budget reimbursements and any cancellation payments separately.
- **People paid by their employer to attend** (executives, managers, board members of funded organisations). No Queensland benchmark document I found addresses this directly. QMHC's rates are written for people with lived/living experience, and HCQ's for consumers. Suggested position: offer each participant a choice. (a) Accept the honorarium personally if taking part in their own time or as a personal contributor. (b) Direct it to their organisation or a nominated community cause. (c) Decline it. Ask them to check their employer's gifts-and-benefits and secondary-employment rules (e.g. Queensland public sector or funded-NGO policies). Inference only; no source verified.
- **QTWAV members paid via Capyble from QTWAV's project funds** creates a perceived self-dealing conflict, because members help decide the project and then receive payment from it. Mitigations: (1) set a uniform, published rate (same $250 for all); (2) QTWAV members declare the conflict, and the payment decision is approved by people who aren't being paid, ideally QC as auspice under its conflict-of-interest policy; (3) record it in the auspice paperwork; (4) separate members' *co-design labour* from their *on-camera interview* and pay each consistently. The ACNC principles above apply if QC is a registered charity (to verify). Running payments through Capyble as supplier doesn't remove the conflict, though it simplifies administration. Be transparent about it in the proposal.
- **Gift cards vs cash/EFT**: default to EFT. Offer a gift card only on request, since for some income-support recipients it's easier to manage. Tell people clearly that a gift card is still likely to count as payment for tax and Centrelink (Justice Connect; HCQ). Don't present gift cards as a way to avoid reporting.
- **Tax/ABN mechanics for Capyble as payer**: at $250 per person (over $75), Capyble should collect for each payee either (a) an ABN, if they take part as a business (consultant, sole trader), and pay against an invoice, with GST only if they're registered; or (b) a completed ATO *Statement by a supplier* (hobby/private). Otherwise Capyble may have to withhold 47%. Whether a one-off filmed interview by a sector professional is a "hobby/private" supply is uncertain. It touches the participant's professional expertise, which leans toward assessable under the ATO graphic-designer example. **Capyble should confirm the approach with its accountant.**
- **GST**: Capyble's honorarium payments to individuals not registered for GST carry no GST (HCQ: sitting fees and gifts are generally not subject to GST). If Capyble is GST-registered, its own invoice to QTWAV/QC for the pass-through honorarium budget probably attracts GST as part of Capyble's taxable supply. Check with an accountant whether to price honoraria as a disbursement or within the fee.
- **Participant information sheet** should say: the amount and what it covers; when it will be paid; that it may be assessable income and may need to be reported to Services Australia; that participants should seek their own advice; and that they may decline payment or redirect it.

## 4. Trauma-informed interviewing practice for filming (pre-brief, grounding, debrief, support contacts, right to stop), including interviews about discrimination or workplace harm

### Takeaway
Guidance agrees on: clear scope and risk information up front; participant control over location, support person, topics and identification; open questions and permission before sensitive topics; an explicit right to pause, skip or stop; a debrief; support at release; and post-publication check-ins. For trans and queer workers, add: don't ask health or body questions, use only the names and pronouns given, and fund or extend EAP-type support.

### Cited Findings
- Our Watch: let survivors choose the interview location and bring a support person; check they have legal and emotional support "throughout the process"; "Respect their right not to talk"; keep them updated on publication timing; suggest they "have a plan for the day the story comes out"; and "Confirm they can access support on the day the story comes out and in the following period." — [Our Watch, Interviewing survivors](https://www.ourwatch.org.au/media-reporting/resources/interviewing-survivors)
- Our Watch trans guide: "Ask – what is on and off the record? Are any areas of discussion off limits? Seek permission before inquiring about sensitive or controversial topics"; no personal health questions; "If in doubt, consider how your questions would sound if you asked them to a cisgender person"; respect cultural protocols (Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander sources; refugee and migrant risk of persecution); extend EAP or fund specialist mental health support; offer QLife; check support networks; check in post-publication; "allowing sources to guide the conversation and share their experiences at their own pace." It also tells media workers to look after themselves and colleagues. — [Our Watch trans guide](https://assets.ourwatch.org.au/assets/Media-resources/OW_Guidelines_reporting_VA_trans_gender_communities_WEB.pdf)
- Our Watch interviewing resources recommend open-ended questions, no assumptions, and discussing identification, retribution, backlash, online abuse and effects on legal proceedings (via Perplexity). — [Our Watch interviewing slides](https://assets.ourwatch.org.au/assets/Media-resources/Interviewing-presentation-slides.pptx)
- If the project counts as research, the NHMRC *National Statement on Ethical Conduct in Human Research* applies, including distress, withdrawal and referral protocols. Perplexity reports a 2025 edition has replaced the 2023 edition (not opened directly). — [NHMRC National Statement 2025 (PDF)](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/publications/National-Statement-on-Ethical-Conduct-Human-Research-2025.pdf)
- Support lines (verify hours before use; via Perplexity): 1800RESPECT 1800 737 732; DVConnect Womensline 1800 811 811 and Mensline 1800 600 636; Lifeline 13 11 14; QLife 1800 184 527 (LGBTIQ+ peer support). — [Our Watch trans guide cites QLife](https://assets.ourwatch.org.au/assets/Media-resources/OW_Guidelines_reporting_VA_trans_gender_communities_WEB.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Proposed filming protocol**:
  1. **Pre-brief call** (paid, about 30 min, 1–2 weeks before): purpose, audience, reuse tiers, visibility choice, topics on and off limits, questions shared in advance, names and pronouns, support person, access needs, safety plan.
  2. **On the day**: quiet private location of their choice or neutral studio; minimal crew; an agreed hand signal and "pause" word; start with low-intensity questions (role, what good looks like); ask permission before discrimination or harm questions; no requests for graphic detail or for naming current employers or colleagues; breaks offered; grounding (water, orientation to the room, feet on floor) offered, not imposed.
  3. **Close**: end on strengths or recommendations, not the hardest material; brief debrief (how are you, what's next today); printed support card (QLife, 1800RESPECT, DVConnect, Lifeline, their EAP); confirm next steps and the review date.
  4. **Aftercare**: check-in within 48 hours; segment review; check-in at publication and about 2 weeks after; named contact for concerns and takedown.
- **Workplace harm content**: participants describing discrimination at a current or identifiable employer risk breaching confidentiality, defamation and employment exposure. The pre-brief should steer toward de-identified, pattern-level accounts, and the edit should remove identifying details. Capyble should reserve the right to cut legally risky content, with legal review if needed.
- **DFSV workers**: vicarious trauma is likely, so avoid soliciting client stories. Focus prompts on organisational practice, which also suits the board/manager audience.
- **Interviewer and crew**: queer/trans-competent interviewer, ideally a QTWAV member or peer if participants want that; crew debrief after each day.
- Doesn't look like formal research, so HREC approval probably isn't required. If outputs are to be published as research (e.g. evaluation papers), consider whether ethics review is needed.

### Gaps
- I didn't open Blue Knot, Phoenix Australia or Dart Centre Asia Pacific guidance directly. The protocol relies on Our Watch (primary) plus general practice synthesis.
- I found no Australian guidance specific to filming DFSV *workers* (as opposed to victim-survivors) about workplace discrimination.

## 5. Data handling: storage, retention and destruction of raw footage and transcripts; using AI transcription safely

### Takeaway
No law sets a retention period for interview footage. APP 11, which binds APP entities and is a sensible baseline for Capyble, requires reasonable security steps and destruction or de-identification once the information is no longer needed. OAIC guidance on commercial AI says to assess the extra risks before putting personal information into AI tools. Capyble's offline-only transcription is the lower-risk option, but local machines, temp files and backups still need controls. The big risk is the hand-over library: unobscured masters of de-identified people should not go to QTWAV.

### Cited Findings
- APP 11 requires an APP entity to take reasonable steps to protect personal information from misuse, interference and loss and from unauthorised access, modification or disclosure, and to destroy or de-identify it when no longer needed for a permitted purpose (unless law requires retention) (via Perplexity). — [OAIC, APP 11 guidelines](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/australian-privacy-principles-guidelines/chapter-11-app-11-security-of-personal-information)
- OAIC guidance on commercially available AI products (Oct 2024) is guidance, not law. It says to assess security risks and APP obligations when selecting AI products, consider whether the vendor uses inputs for training, and be cautious about entering personal (especially sensitive) information into publicly available AI tools (via Perplexity). — [OAIC, Guidance on privacy and the use of commercially available AI products](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/guidance-on-privacy-and-the-use-of-commercially-available-ai-products)
- The Notifiable Data Breaches scheme applies only to entities covered by the Privacy Act. Small businesses are generally outside it unless an s 6D exception applies (via Perplexity). — [OAIC, Notifiable Data Breaches](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/notifiable-data-breaches)
- NHMRC *Management of Data and Information in Research*: for research, the general minimum retention is 5 years from publication, varying by type (12 months for some short projects). It applies to research only, not to training production (via Perplexity). — [NHMRC guide (PDF)](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/attachments/Management-of-Data-and-Information-in-Research.pdf)
- The statutory privacy tort (from 10 June 2025) can apply to misuse of information where there's a reasonable expectation of privacy and the invasion is serious, regardless of turnover. — [POLA Act 2024, Sch 2](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2024A00128/latest/text)
- ABC: removing names or pixelating may not de-identify. — [ABC privacy guidance note](https://www.abc.net.au/edpols/privacy/105747778)

### Inferences
- **Proposed data position**:
  - *Storage*: raw footage and audio on encrypted drives (and an encrypted offsite backup), limited to named Capyble staff. No consumer cloud sync for raw files. Consent forms and the name-to-pseudonym key are stored separately from footage.
  - *AI use*: transcription and tagging only, with offline tools on an encrypted Capyble machine (e.g. a local Whisper-class model) with telemetry off. Delete intermediate files (audio extracts, temp files) after QA. No footage, transcripts or likenesses go to cloud AI services or are used for training models, voice cloning or synthetic avatars. State this explicitly in the consent form; it's a growing participant concern. Humans check transcripts before anything is quoted.
  - *Retention*: keep raw rushes until module sign-off plus a short buffer (e.g. 3 months), then destroy and log it. Keep final edited clips (with de-identification baked in) and transcripts of used material for the module's life.
  - *Library hand-over to QTWAV/QC*: hand over **only final, consent-checked clips** tagged with each contributor's reuse tier and consent expiry. Don't hand over raw footage of anyone at visibility levels 3–4. Raw footage of level 1–2 contributors goes over only if the contributor ticked Tier 2. Include a consent register and a takedown procedure. QC becomes custodian and should apply its own privacy policy. Confirm QC's capacity, e.g. secure storage and a named custodian, since QTWAV is unincorporated.
  - *Destruction*: secure deletion of all Capyble copies once the hand-over is confirmed, apart from what Capyble needs for module maintenance, with a destruction log available on request.
  - *Breach*: even if Capyble isn't formally covered by the NDB scheme, commit contractually to tell QC/QTWAV and affected participants promptly about any loss or unauthorised access.
- If Capyble ever uses a cloud transcription service, do a documented assessment first (vendor training use, data location, deletion) and get participant consent. Simplest is to commit to offline-only in the proposal.

- I found no DFSV-sector or LGBTIQ+-sector standard on retention periods for training-video footage.
