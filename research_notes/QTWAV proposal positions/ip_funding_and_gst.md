## 1. Auspicing: who should sign the contract and hold the IP?

### Takeaway
QTWAV is unincorporated, so it cannot contract in its own name and cannot reliably hold property such as copyright. The contract, and an assignment of the IP, should be with **Queensland Council for LGBTI Health (ABN 58 039 823 994)** as auspice. The contract should name QTWAV as the project or community the work is for. It should also record that QC holds the IP for QTWAV's benefit under their auspice arrangement. The Justice Connect guide says the auspice agreement should state who owns project IP during the arrangement and **after it ends**. Capyble should ask QC what its auspice agreement with QTWAV says.

### Cited findings
- Justice Connect's *Auspicing guide* (Oct 2025) on unincorporated associations: "an unincorporated association can't enter into contracts in its own name and it creates doubt as to who can enter into contracts on behalf of the association". It also says uncertain membership "may lead to uncertainty about who is the legal owner of any property of the unincorporated association" (p. ~30). — [Justice Connect NFP Law, Auspicing guide (PDF, Oct 2025)](https://content.nfplaw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Auspicing-guide.pdf)
- The same guide warns that unincorporated members may be treated as a **partnership** and be jointly liable for debts. — [Auspicing guide](https://content.nfplaw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Auspicing-guide.pdf)
- In an auspice arrangement, the auspicor receives the project funding, enters into the relevant agreements for the auspicee, and usually keeps the delivery obligations. The page compares auspicing to sub-contracting. — [Justice Connect, What is auspicing?](https://www.nfplaw.org.au/auspicing)
- Guide, IP section: "the parties need to determine who owns the intellectual property produced as part of the arrangement and the way that intellectual property can be used by both parties. This should cover the time during auspicing as well as after it has ended. For instance, the auspicee may be entitled to the ownership of the intellectual property, but the auspicor may be provided with a licence to use the intellectual property to promote their involvement." — [Auspicing guide, p. 21](https://content.nfplaw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Auspicing-guide.pdf)
- The guide also says parties should consider registering created IP where "the relevant intellectual property is intended to be used as part of the activities of either party after the completion of the project". — [Auspicing guide, p. 21](https://content.nfplaw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Auspicing-guide.pdf)
- The guide's checklist covers these areas:
  - **Obligations after termination:** "intellectual property and what is to happen to any remaining grant money (bearing in mind the grant agreement may require return of surplus)".
  - **Parties:** if the auspicee is an unincorporated association, "an individual member (or members)… will be required to enter into the agreement as representative of, and on behalf of, all the individuals".
  - **End of project:** parties should agree how to distribute surplus funds "taking into account the terms of the funding agreement".
  — [Auspicing guide, pp. 22, 27–29](https://content.nfplaw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Auspicing-guide.pdf)
- The guide says background IP can stay with each party, licensed "exclusive or non-exclusive… on a fee or royalty free basis" for the agreed purpose. — [Auspicing guide, p. 21](https://content.nfplaw.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Auspicing-guide.pdf)
- An example of the "auspicee owns, auspicor gets a licence" model from another sector: Landcare Victoria's policy says member groups keep ownership of IP created by the project, and the auspicor gets permission to use it to promote its involvement. — [Landcare Victoria Member Group Grants Policy (2022)](https://www.landcarevictoria.org.au/common/Uploaded%20files/File%20storage/Rules,%20Policies,%20Procedures/Member%20Group%20Grants%20Policy%20and%20Procedure_Landcare%20Victoria%20Inc_12.08.2022.pdf)
- ABN Lookup details for QC:
  - Legal name: QUEENSLAND COUNCIL FOR LGBTI HEALTH, ABN 58 039 823 994, "Other Incorporated Entity". It was formerly Queensland AIDS Council Inc and took its current legal name on 30 Oct 2024.
  - Business names: "Queensland Council for LGBTI Health" and "QCGP". **QTWAV is not a registered business name of QC.**
  — [ABN Lookup – current](https://abr.business.gov.au/ABN/View/58039823994); [ABN history](https://abr.business.gov.au/AbnHistory/View/58039823994)

### Inferences
- **Contracting party:** "Queensland Council for LGBTI Health (ABN 58 039 823 994), as auspice for Queer and Trans Workers Against Violence (QTWAV)". QTWAV's committee should still approve scope and sign off deliverables, for example through a named QTWAV representative in a project-governance clause. The signatory and payer should be QC. Capyble should check that the QC signatory has delegated authority for a $40k purchase.
- **IP clause in Capyble's contract (⚖️ draft with a lawyer):**
  1. Capyble assigns copyright in the bespoke deliverables to QC on payment (or on creation). The assignment should be "to be held for the benefit of QTWAV in accordance with the auspice agreement between QC and QTWAV".
  2. Capyble keeps its background IP: templates, frameworks, methods, code and stock assets. It grants QC a perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free, sub-licensable licence to use that background IP as embedded in the deliverables. The sub-licence right matters because QC must in turn license the State (see section 2).
  3. QC grants Capyble a licence back for portfolio, case-study and awards use, with no public-facing or participant-identifying footage unless consented.
  4. Capyble staff give a moral-rights consent covering edits and adaptations.
- **Making the content outlast QTWAV and the funded role:** ask QC to confirm, or add to its auspice agreement, what happens to the IP if QTWAV winds up, incorporates or changes auspice. Options include transfer to an incorporated QTWAV, transfer to a nominated LGBTIQ+ or DFV NFP, or QC keeping it under an open licence. An open licence (section 3) is the most robust safeguard, because it survives even if the owner disappears.
- Capyble should not contract with individual QTWAV members. That brings in the uncertainty and partnership-liability risks Justice Connect describes.

### Gaps

For Queensland, the equivalent is the *Associations Incorporation Act 1981* (Qld). I did not verify this further.
- QC's actual auspice agreement or MOU with QTWAV is not public. Capyble needs to ask for its IP and end-of-auspice terms.
- I did not establish whether QTWAV plans to incorporate before October 2027.

## 2. Queensland Government funding-agreement terms: IP, acknowledgement, subcontracting, unspent funds

### Takeaway
Under the standard terms:
- IP in material created "by You or on Your behalf" (which includes Capyble's work for QC) **vests in QC**, and QC must give the **State a perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free, worldwide, sub-licensable licence** to use, publish, adapt and modify it.
- Subcontracting any part of the Services needs the department's **prior written consent**.
- Funding must be acknowledged with a departmental logo.
- Unspent funding is handled through reporting. The department can let the organisation **retain and spend** it on approved terms, **reduce future payments**, or **require repayment**.
- The terms say "unspent", not "uncommitted". **A signed contract with Capyble does not, on the face of the terms, protect underspend.** QC needs written authorisation from the department to use the year-one underspend for this project.

### Cited findings
- The standard terms are **Version 1.2, dated 21 July 2025**, the latest version I found. They are standard terms "upon which funding is granted under social service programs administered by the Queensland Government". — [Service Agreement – Standard Terms v1.2 (forgov.qld.gov.au)](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)
- **IP ownership (cl 16.1):**
  - "Intellectual Property Rights in Your Material vest in You."
  - "Your Material" means "any information, document, electronic image, cinematographic film, video, equipment, software or data… created by You **or on Your behalf** in performing Services" (cl 31.1).
  — [Standard Terms v1.2](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)
- **Licence to the State (cl 16.2(a)):** "You grant to the State of Queensland a perpetual, irrevocable, royalty-free, worldwide and non-exclusive licence (including a right to sub-licence) to use, communicate, reproduce, publish, adapt, and modify Your Material and any Existing Material." — [Standard Terms v1.2](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)
- **"Existing Material"** (pre-existing or independently produced material incorporated in Your Material) is also licensed to the State. **Warranty (cl 16.4(a)):** the funded organisation warrants it is "entitled… to deal with the Intellectual Property Rights in Your Material and any Existing Material" in that way. — [Standard Terms v1.2](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)
- **Further assurance (cl 16.3):** if requested, the organisation must sign documents to give effect to clause 16. **Moral rights (cl 16.5(b)):** the organisation and its subcontractors must not infringe IP or moral rights. — [Standard Terms v1.2](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)
- **Definition quirk:** "Intellectual Property Rights" "includes copyright, **except for copyright in 'artistic works'** as… defined in the Copyright Act 1968" and excludes moral rights (cl 31.1). — [Standard Terms v1.2](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)
- **Acknowledgement (cl 10):** funding must be acknowledged in the annual report and "promotional materials relating to the Services", and "Any acknowledgement in promotional material… must use an acknowledgement logo which You must obtain from Us." — [Standard Terms v1.2](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)
- **Subcontracting (cl 22.1):** "You must not, without Our prior written consent, subcontract the whole, or any part, of Your obligations". The department may impose conditions and ask for a copy of the subcontract, and cl 22.2 makes the funded organisation responsible for subcontractor suitability. **Cl 12.6:** subcontracts must give the department audit access to the subcontractor's records "to the same extent" as the funded organisation. — [Standard Terms v1.2](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)
- **Use of funding (cl 7.1(a)):** funding may only be used to deliver the Services in the Funding Schedule, "within any time period stipulated… for expenditure of the Funding". Misuse can be recovered as a debt (cl 7.1(c)). — [Standard Terms v1.2](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)
- **Unspent amounts each period (cl 7.3):** if a report shows funding "for the previous period is unspent", the department may:
  - "(a) authorise You to retain the unspent Funding and to expend some or all of it for an approved purpose, on terms and conditions specified by Us";
  - "(b) reduce a future payment…"; or
  - "(c) notify You that You are required to repay the unspent Funding" (not less than 20 business days).
  — [Standard Terms v1.2](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)
- **At expiry (cl 7.4):** any funding "not been spent" at the end of the Funding Schedule or agreement may be recalled, or retention may be authorised. Repayment follows any show-cause process required under a Governing Act. **The word "committed" does not appear in the standard terms in relation to funding.** I ran a text search of the full v1.2 document. — [Standard Terms v1.2](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)
- **Earnings (cl 7.2):** interest and fees earned from the Services must be treated as Funding. **Separate ledger (cl 7.5)** is required. — [Standard Terms v1.2](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)
- A Department of Families, Seniors, Disability Services and Child Safety (DFSDSCS) funding "particulars" template refers to an **Unspent Funding Report** that the funded organisation must submit. I saw this in a search snippet only and did not open the document. — [DFSDSCS particulars (non-service provision) docx](https://www.families.qld.gov.au/_media/documents/about-us/funding-grants/agreements/particulars-non-service-provision.docx)
- **Which department:** Perplexity reports that DFSDSCS administers DFSV funding after the post-October-2024 machinery-of-government changes, and that the Standard Suite of Contracts applies. **Not verified**: the families.qld.gov.au pages returned 403 to my fetch. — [DFSDSCS DFSV funding and investment](https://www.families.qld.gov.au/our-work/domestic-family-sexual-violence/for-service-providers/funding-investment); [Social services agreements and contracts](https://www.families.qld.gov.au/about-us/our-department/funding-grants-investment/social-services-agreements-contracts)

### Inferences
- **IP chain to protect:** Capyble → QC (assignment) → State (licence under cl 16.2) and → public (any open licence). Under cl 16.4(a), QC warrants it can license the State, so QC's lawyers will likely require an assignment or a very broad sub-licensable licence from Capyble. Capyble should plan for:
  - any **background IP** embedded in the deliverables (templates, illustrations, music, stock footage) being licensed onward to the State perpetually and with a right to modify;
  - excluding, or clearly ring-fencing, third-party stock with restrictive licences;
  - building the cost of stock music and footage licences that permit sub-licensing into the quote.
- **Artistic works:** because "Intellectual Property Rights" excludes copyright in artistic works, illustrations and graphics may technically fall outside the State's IP licence, while films and text are covered. Capyble's own contract should still deal with artistic works expressly. ⚖️
- **Subcontracting:** whether Capyble's engagement counts as "subcontracting the Services" depends on how QC's Funding Schedule describes the Services. If the e-learning is itself a funded output, QC probably needs the department's prior written consent. **Ask QC to confirm consent or that none is needed before signing.** Capyble should also expect an audit-access clause (cl 12.6) in its contract.
- **Acknowledgement:** the modules and videos are likely "promotional materials relating to the Services". Plan for the departmental acknowledgement logo plus QTWAV, QC and Snow Foundation/Capyble credits on the title and credits screens. Budget time for department logo approval.
- **Underspend: biggest timing risk.** QTWAV's $40k is first-year underspend. Under cl 7.3, once it is reported as unspent, the department decides whether QC may keep it. **Capyble should ask QC for the department's written authorisation under cl 7.3(a)**, or confirmation that the underspend sits within an open multi-year Funding Schedule with no period-end acquittal. Capyble should also ask about **any spend-by date** in that authorisation. A signed Capyble contract ("committed") is not recognised in the standard terms. If the department has not yet approved retention, the contract should allow termination without penalty, and payment for work done to date, if the funding is withdrawn.
- **Hard backstop:** the grant ends **October 2027**. Under cl 7.4, any money unspent at expiry may be recalled. The July 2027 final delivery leaves about three months' buffer, so slippage must not push the final invoice past the Funding Schedule end.

### Gaps
- QC's actual Funding Schedule, Service Agreement form (standard vs short form), funding periods (financial year vs grant year) and reporting dates are unknown. These determine whether "first-year underspend" is already reported and whether the period ends 30 June.
- I could not find or open the DFV-specific Investment Specification for workforce development, so any extra IP or branding requirements there are unverified.
- I could not verify on families.qld.gov.au which department funds QC's DFSV workforce work.

## 3. Open licensing so the resources outlast QTWAV

### Takeaway
Queensland Government policy (QGEA v5.0.0, Dec 2025) makes **CC BY 4.0** the default for State-owned information. ANROWS commonly publishes under **CC BY-NC**. Our Watch **reserves copyright**. Options for QTWAV:
- **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike):** keeps adaptations open and stops commercial resale. It also stops Capyble, and anyone charging for training, from commercial reuse without separate permission.
- **CC BY 4.0:** matches the State's default and maximises reuse.

Whichever licence is chosen, CC licences are irrevocable. **The raw footage library of identifiable queer and trans DFSV workers should probably not be openly licensed.**

### Cited findings
- **QGEA *Open data, information sharing, access and use policy*** (Final v5.0.0; "Effective: December 2025–current"; mandated):
  - "Agencies must apply a consistent and clear information licensing framework to government information, with the default being the least restrictive Creative Commons licence"
  - "Use Creative Commons licenses version 4.0 (international licence)… The default license should be the least restrictive, CC Attribution (CC BY), to maximise the potential for reuse and innovation."
  - The policy applies "where copyright is owned by the State of Queensland".
  — [forgov.qld.gov.au – QGEA open data policy](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/information-technology/queensland-government-enterprise-architecture-qgea/qgea-directions-and-guidance/qgea-policies-standards-and-guidelines/open-data-information-sharing-access-and-use-policy) (verified by direct fetch)
- The Queensland public sector IP principles also say CC BY is the default, assessed case by case. This comes from Perplexity's summary and was not separately fetched. — [Qld public sector IP principles](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/information-technology/queensland-government-enterprise-architecture-qgea/qgea-directions-and-guidance/qgea-policies-standards-and-guidelines/queensland-government-public-sector-intellectual-property-principles)
- **ANROWS** uses "Attribution-Non Commercial (CC BY-NC 4.0 International)" on current publications, and CC BY-NC 3.0 AU on older ones. ANROWS branding, third-party content and trademarks are excluded. — [ANROWS NCAS 2017 findings](https://www.anrows.org.au/publication/australians-attitudes-to-violence-against-women-and-gender-equality-findings-from-the-2017-national-community-attitudes-towards-violence-against-women-survey-ncas/read/); [ANROWS evaluation summary](https://www.anrows.org.au/publication/rante-rante-ampe-marle-and-urreye-safe-respected-and-free-from-violence-projects-evaluation/read-summary/)
- **Our Watch** "retains copyright for all the content on this site". Use requires written consent, except educational use by educational institutions and personal non-commercial use. A separate legal-statements page says it "consent[s] to the use of, the dissemination, and re-use and distribution of Our Watch materials". The two pages are inconsistent. — [Our Watch copyright](https://www.ourwatch.org.au/copyright); [Our Watch privacy and legal statements](https://www.ourwatch.org.au/privacy-and-legal-statements)
- **Safe and Equal, Full Stop Australia and DV-alert:** I found no verified Creative Commons licence on their training resources (Perplexity search, unconfirmed absence).

### Inferences
- **Recommended position** (Capyble can propose; QTWAV and QC decide):
  - **Finished modules and videos:** published under **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0**, or **CC BY-NC 4.0** to align with ANROWS. QC/QTWAV is the licensor. Department logos, the QC/QTWAV marks and third-party stock are carved out.
  - **NonCommercial:** keeps the material free for the sector, while stopping a private RTO from repackaging and selling it.
  - **ShareAlike:** adaptations by other services stay open.
  - **CC BY 4.0** suits QTWAV if maximum uptake is the goal (including by paid trainers) and it wants to mirror State policy. The State already gets a broad licence under cl 16.2 whatever QTWAV chooses.
- **Footage library (raw clips):** do **not** CC-license it publicly. CC licences cannot be revoked, and people filmed (queer and trans workers, possibly lived-experience participants) may later need to withdraw for safety reasons. Instead, keep raw footage owned by QC for QTWAV under a **restricted licence**, used only by QTWAV and approved partners for DFSV training. Talent releases should state permitted uses, duration and a take-down process. Only edited, consented clips should go in the open modules. ⚖️ (privacy and consent wording)
- **Capyble's portfolio rights:** under an NC licence, Capyble cannot reuse the content commercially, e.g. in paid training for other clients. Capyble therefore needs an **express licence-back** for portfolio and showcase use, and must keep ownership of its background templates and frameworks. Showing excerpts in a portfolio is likely non-commercial or covered by the licence-back, but should be stated expressly.
- **Hosting and source files:** for the content to outlast QTWAV, deliverables should include:
  - **editable source files** (authoring-tool project files, video project files, captions/SRT, transcripts);
  - **SCORM/xAPI exports** and **MP4 masters**;
  - a hosting arrangement on an account **owned by QC/QTWAV** (not Capyble's LMS or video account);
  - a handover register of all third-party asset licences.

  If any source format depends on a paid authoring tool, note that QTWAV needs a licence to edit it later.

- I did not verify Safe and Equal's licence directly.

## 4. GST: is QC registered, can it claim credits, and is "$40,000 incl. GST" the right framing?

### Takeaway
QC is **GST-registered (since 1 July 2000)** and is a PBI charity with DGR status. As a GST-registered entity buying services for its enterprise, QC should generally be able to claim back the GST on Capyble's invoice as an input tax credit. That makes **QC's real cost the GST-exclusive amount**. Queensland funding is also expressed **exclusive of GST**, with GST paid on top. So QTWAV's "$40,000" is quite possibly a **GST-exclusive budget figure**. If Capyble quotes $40,000 including GST ($36,364 ex GST), it may leave about **$3,600** on the table. **Ask QC's finance team whether the $40k is ex or inc GST.**

### Cited findings
- **ABN Lookup:** QC is "GST: Registered from 01 Jul 2000". It is an ACNC-registered charity (from 3 Dec 2012), subtype **Public Benevolent Institution**, with GST concession (from 1 Jul 2005), FBT exemption and income tax exemption. It is endorsed as a **DGR** under Item 1 of s 30-15 ITAA 1997. — [ABN Lookup](https://abr.business.gov.au/ABN/View/58039823994)
- **Standard Terms cl 9.1:**
  - "The amount of the Funding as set out in a Funding Schedule is **exclusive of GST**."
  - "We will pay You the GST Amount at the same time as paying You the Funding if You are registered for GST."
  - Cl 9.4 provides for recipient-created tax invoices (RCTIs), and cl 9.5 requires the GST to be remitted to the ATO.
  — [Standard Terms v1.2](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)
- **Input tax credits:** a registered entity can claim credits for acquisitions made for a creditable purpose, i.e. in carrying on its enterprise and not relating to input-taxed supplies (GST Act ss 11-5, 11-15). Charitable or grant-funded status does not by itself deny the credit. Source: Perplexity summary citing the GST Act; statute not re-read this session. — [A New Tax System (Goods and Services Tax) Act 1999](https://www.legislation.gov.au/details/C2014C00008)

### Inferences
- **Framing in the quote:** show **ex-GST, GST and total** on every line and milestone, and quote ex GST first: "$X + GST". Three outcomes for QC:
  1. **QTWAV's $40k is ex GST:** Capyble can quote up to $40,000 + $4,000 GST. QC's net cost is $40k, recovered through its GST credit.
  2. **QC confirms $40k is a hard cash cap including GST:** quote $36,364 + $3,636. QC's net cost is $36,364, so QTWAV effectively has about $3,636 "spare". It is worth pointing this out to QC.
  3. **Cash flow:** QC pays the GST up front and claims it back on its BAS. This is minor, but it is why some NFPs budget cash inc GST.
- PBI status matters for FBT and DGR, not for whether QC can claim credits on this purchase. 🧾 Confirm with QC's finance team that the project is not tied to input-taxed activities. It is very unlikely to be.

### Gaps
- I do not know QC's internal budgeting convention or whether QTWAV's committee figure came from a GST-exclusive budget line.

## 5. GST treatment of the "community contribution" / discount and the Snow Foundation subsidy

### Takeaway
If the community contribution is shown on the tax invoice as a genuine **discount**, GST is payable only on the **net price actually charged**. A full-value line followed by a "community contribution" deduction is fine, provided the invoice clearly shows the amount payable and the GST on it. The main GST risk is the Snow Foundation. A **general grant** to Capyble is not consideration for the supply to QC. If Snow's funding is **tied to** subsidising specific services to specific clients, part of it could be **third-party consideration**, and GST could apply to that part. Wording matters: "Capyble community contribution, supported by the Snow Foundation", **not** "Snow Foundation pays $6,000 of this invoice". 🧾 Confirm with Capyble's accountant against the actual Snow Foundation grant agreement.

### Cited findings
- **Taxable supply:** under GST Act s 9-5, a taxable supply needs consideration, and "consideration" is broad and includes payments by third parties (s 9-15). A genuine discount allowed at or before supply reduces consideration, so GST is 1/11 of the discounted GST-inclusive price. Wholly free (pro bono) services with no consideration are ordinarily not a taxable supply. — Perplexity summary citing [GST Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/details/C2014C00008). ⚠️ Secondary summary; ATO pages not directly fetched.
- **GSTR 2012/2 (financial assistance payments):** a payment is consideration for a supply where there is a "sufficient nexus", i.e. it is made "in connection with", "in response to" or "for the inducement of" a supply (an objective test). Payments made for support or aid without a supply in return may involve no supply. — [ATO GSTR 2012/2](https://www.ato.gov.au/law/view/document?locid='GST/GSTR20122/NAT/ATO/fp74'&PiT=99991231235958) (summary via Perplexity, and secondary summary at [Communities in Control – GST on grants](https://communitiesincontrol.com.au/help-sheets/gst-on-grants)). ⚠️ ATO page returned 403 to direct fetch, so paragraph numbers are not verified.
- **Tripartite arrangements:** Ashurst reports the ATO's view that incentive payments may be consideration for a supply to a third party "depending on the specific facts and circumstances". — [Ashurst GST bulletin, Dec 2013](https://www.ashurst.com/-/media/ashurst/documents/news-and-insights/legal-updates/2013/dec/gst_bulletin_-_10_december_2013.pdf) (older material)
- **ATO, government funding:** grant funding has no GST implications where it is not for a supply. GST may arise where the recipient provides something in return. — [ATO GST and grants (government entities)](https://www.ato.gov.au/businesses-and-organisations/corporate-tax-measures-and-assurance/government-entities/gst-for-government/gst-and-grants) (via Perplexity)
- **Tax invoices:** a tax invoice must show enough to identify the supply, the price and the GST, and invoices of $1,000 or more must also identify the buyer or ABN (GST Act s 29-70). — via Perplexity citing the [GST Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/details/C2014C00008). ⚠️ Not re-verified on ato.gov.au this session.

### Inferences
- **Recommended invoice and quote layout (🧾 confirm):**

  | Line | Ex GST |
  |---|---:|
  | Project fees at Capyble standard rates | $46,364 (example) |
  | Less: Capyble community contribution, about 20% of staff time, supported by the Snow Foundation | ($6,364) |
  | **Fee payable** | **$40,000** |
  | GST 10% (on fee payable) | $4,000 |
  | **Total payable** | **$44,000** |

  This assumes $40k ex GST. Under the inc-GST framing: fee payable $36,364 + GST $3,636 = $40,000.

  - Compute GST only on the net fee payable.
  - Do not describe the contribution as a "donation" or "gift" to QC. A discount on a commercial supply is not a gift, and QC is a DGR, so "gift" language could confuse receipting.
  - Keep timesheet and rate records supporting the stated value.
- **Snow Foundation wording:** describe the subsidy as Capyble's own discount, made possible by Capyble's general Snow Foundation support. Do **not** describe it as a payment by Snow toward QC's invoice, and do not invoice Snow for it.
- **If Snow's grant is tied to specific deliverables:** if the grant agreement specifies delivering subsidised work to named community clients, ask the accountant whether some of it is third-party consideration. That would make GST payable on it, likely from within the Snow grant rather than charged to QC.
- **QC's acquittal:** QC may want to report the in-kind value to the department as a leveraged contribution. Offer to give QC a short letter stating the value and basis of the contribution. A letter is better than putting it on the tax invoice as a separate taxable item.

### Gaps
- No ATO public guidance specifically addresses showing pro bono value on invoices. The "not a tax invoice" and value-statement suggestions are good practice, not ATO rules.
- The terms of Capyble's Snow Foundation grant are unknown, and they determine the GST outcome.

## 6. Presenting the in-kind contribution, Snow Foundation norms, and payment milestones vs 30 June

### Takeaway
Australian pro bono practice is to show the **standard value, then the contribution or discount, then the amount payable**, supported by time and rate records. This suits a quote line such as "Community contribution (auspiced, community-owned organisation)". I found no published Snow Foundation acknowledgement guidelines for grantees. On milestones, the proposed 50/25/25 schedule puts **75% ($30k) in FY2026–27 and 25% after 30 June 2027**. Whether that is a problem depends on the spend-by terms of the department's underspend authorisation.

### Cited findings
- **Pro bono presentation:** standard fee, then pro bono or in-kind discount, then amount payable, labelled "pro bono discount", "fee waived" or "in-kind contribution". The value is recorded by reference to the standard fee and time. — Perplexity summary attributing this to the [Australian Pro Bono Centre](https://www.probonocentre.org.au/). ⚠️ No specific APBC page verified; treat as general practice.
- **Snow Foundation:** it runs the "Snow Entrepreneurs" social-enterprise fellowship (launched Oct 2021), with grants of up to $100,000 in year one plus mentoring, for leaders working in the **ACT or NSW**. — [Snow Foundation media release, 6 Oct 2021](https://www.snowfoundation.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/SNOW-ENTREPRENEURS-MR-06-Oct-2021-1.pdf); [Social Impact Hub](https://www.socialimpacthub.org/news/best-practice-philanthropy-snow-foundation) (older material)
- **Unspent funds:** standard terms cl 7.3 and 7.4 (see section 2) tie unspent funds to period reporting and expiry. — [Standard Terms v1.2](https://www.forgov.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0028/452782/updatedserviceagreementstandardterms.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Quote presentation:** list a "Community contribution: about 20% of Capyble staff time (about $6,000–6,500 ex GST), because QTWAV is an auspiced, community-owned network". Add a footnote that Capyble's community work is supported by the Snow Foundation, if Capyble's grant terms allow or require that credit. Capyble should check its own Snow Foundation grant letter for acknowledgement requirements; I found none published. Offer an optional credit in the module end-screens, e.g. "Produced by Capyble with support from the Snow Foundation". It must sit alongside the mandatory Queensland Government acknowledgement logo (cl 10).
- **Milestones against the 30 June financial year:**
  - **50% at kick-off (late Nov 2026):** strong. It moves underspend into "spent" status quickly, which is the best protection under cl 7.3 and 7.4, where "committed" is not recognised.
  - **25% at module 1 draft (May 2027):** lands before 30 June 2027. Good.
  - **25% at final delivery (July 2027):** lands in FY2027–28. **Risk:** if the department authorised retention of the underspend for spending by 30 June 2027, or QC's funding period is a financial year, QC may struggle to pay it.
  - **Options:**
    1. Bring the third milestone forward to "module 2 draft / all content delivered for review", invoiced by mid-June 2027, with a small (e.g. 10%) final-acceptance payment in July.
    2. Get QC's written confirmation that the funds can be spent until the grant ends in October 2027.
    3. Re-weight to 50/30/20.
  - **Also include:** a clause that if QC's funding is withdrawn, Capyble is paid for work done; and a long-stop date well before the October 2027 grant end.
- Invoices should go to QC (not QTWAV), quote QC's ABN, reference "QTWAV e-learning project", and probably need a QC purchase order number.

### Gaps
