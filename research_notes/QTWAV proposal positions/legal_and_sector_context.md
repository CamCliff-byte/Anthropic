## 1. Federal law: SDA positive duty, AHRC enforcement, gender identity and intersex protections, Tickle v Giggle, special measures and exemptions, Fair Work Act

### Takeaway
The federal law is settled and now binding. The SDA s 47C positive duty applies to every Queensland employer and PCBU, and it is the only operative anti-discrimination positive duty in Queensland. *Giggle for Girls v Tickle* is now final: the Full Federal Court ruled on 15 May 2026 and the High Court refused special leave on 10 September 2026. The case establishes that excluding a trans woman from a women-only service because she is trans can be **direct** gender identity discrimination. A special measure "for women" under s 7D does not, by itself, answer a gender identity claim. For DFV services, the case is about a women-only app, not a refuge, so it is a strong but not identical precedent.

### Cited Findings
- **Positive duty (SDA s 47C).** It commenced **13 December 2022**. Employers and PCBUs must take "reasonable and proportionate measures" to eliminate, as far as possible, sex discrimination, sexual harassment, sex-based harassment, hostile workplace environments on the ground of sex, and related victimisation, including by workers and third parties. It is enforced by the AHRC, not by individual complaint. — Perplexity summary citing [legislation.gov.au SDA compilation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A02868/2025-11-01/2025-11-01/text/original/pdf) (secondary; humanrights.gov.au blocked)
- **AHRC enforcement powers.** The AHRC has been able to enforce the duty since **12 December 2023**, under AHRC Act Part II Div 4A. Its powers are inquiries, compliance notices, enforceable undertakings, and Federal Court applications to enforce compliance notices. — [QHRC news, via search summary](https://www.qhrc.qld.gov.au/our-work/news-and-events/news/news-anti-discrimination-act-reforms-delayed) and Perplexity summary (secondary)
- **No public enforcement found.** I found no publicly reported AHRC compliance notice or enforceable undertaking against a named organisation between December 2023 and October 2026. This does not prove none exist; confidential activity is possible. — Perplexity search (gap; not verified at humanrights.gov.au)
- **Grounds of discrimination.** The SDA has protected **sexual orientation, gender identity and intersex status** since the 2013 amendments (s 5A–5C). — Perplexity summary (secondary; this matches long-standing law)
- **Tickle v Giggle, first instance (Bromwich J, 23 Aug 2024).** The Federal Court found **indirect** gender identity discrimination, awarded $10,000, and rejected the constitutional challenge to the gender identity provisions. — [Wikipedia: Tickle v Giggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tickle_v_Giggle); [ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-05-15/nsw-tickle-v-giggle-judgement-transgender-woman/106682498)
- **Full Federal Court appeal, *Giggle for Girls Pty Ltd v Tickle* [2026] FCAFC 64 (15 May 2026; Perry, Abraham and Kennett JJ).** The Court dismissed the appeal and allowed the cross-appeal.
  - It found **two acts of direct discrimination**: removing Tickle from the app, and refusing to restore her access.
  - It increased damages to **$20,000**, citing aggravating conduct.
  - The correct comparator is a **cisgender woman**. Comparing with a cisgender man would "re-enact that discrimination and invalidate the gender identity" of a trans woman (Perry J).
  - Knowledge of trans status is not a necessary element of direct discrimination.
  - — [Victorian Government Solicitor's Office summary, 2 June 2026](https://www.vgso.vic.gov.au/full-federal-court-hands-down-landmark-decision-gender-identity-discrimination); [ABC, 15 May 2026](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-05-15/nsw-tickle-v-giggle-judgement-transgender-woman/106682498); [QNews](https://qnews.com.au/federal-court-appeal-fails-in-giggle-v-tickle-discrimination-case/)
- **Costs: the sources conflict.** News reports say appeal costs were capped at about $50,000 ([Daily Declaration](https://dailydeclaration.org.au/2026/05/15/giggle-v-tickle-sall-grover-loses/)). VGSO says costs are "up to $100,000" ([VGSO](https://www.vgso.vic.gov.au/full-federal-court-hands-down-landmark-decision-gender-identity-discrimination)). Not material to the modules.
- **s 7D special measures.** The Full Court reportedly adopted a **"distributive" reading**: a special measure for men and women does not answer a gender identity claim. — [search summary of AHRC release and news](https://humanrights.gov.au/about-us/media-centre/media-releases/sex-and-gender-rights/full-federal-court-finds-two-acts-of-direct-discrimination-in-giggle-v-tickle-appeal) (secondary; AHRC page returned 403, and the judgment text was not read directly)
- **High Court special leave refused, 10 September 2026, with costs** (*Giggle for Girls Pty Ltd v Tickle* [2026] HCADisp 209; S46/2026). The Court said: "Any appeal dependent on the demonstration of error in the Full Court's construction of those provisions has insufficient prospects of success." — [HCA disposition page](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/cases-and-judgments/judgments/special-leave-dispositions/giggle-girls-pty-ltd-acn-632-152-017-anor-v-tickle) (503 when fetched; wording via search summary); [PinkNews, 11 Sep 2026](https://www.thepinknews.com/2026/09/11/final-high-court-decision-giggle-for-girls-v-tickle-trans-discrimination-case/)
- **Campaigning continues.** After the refusal, the respondent's campaign has moved to seeking parliamentary amendment of the SDA. — [search summary of news](https://www.thepinknews.com/2026/09/11/final-high-court-decision-giggle-for-girls-v-tickle-trans-discrimination-case/) (secondary)
- **No legislative change found.** No federal amendment removing or narrowing the SDA gender identity protections, and no enacted federal Human Rights Act, was identified as of October 2026. — Perplexity search of legislation.gov.au (secondary; a gap if a bill was introduced recently)
- **Other SDA exemptions.** These are framed around **sex**, not gender identity:
  - s 30: genuine occupational qualifications.
  - s 32: services that "can only be provided to members of one sex".
  - ss 37–38: religious bodies.
  - s 44: AHRC temporary exemptions.
  - No blanket exemption for women-only DFV services was identified. — Perplexity summary (secondary; check the exact text of s 30/s 32 before the modules quote it)
- **Fair Work Act 2009.**
  - s 351 (adverse action) and s 772 (unlawful termination) include sexual orientation, gender identity and intersex status. In my recollection the last two were added by the Secure Jobs, Better Pay Act 2022 (unverified).
  - The Part 3-5A prohibition on workplace sexual harassment (s 527D) commenced **6 March 2023**, with Fair Work Commission stop-sexual-harassment orders.
  - — Perplexity summary citing [legislation.gov.au Fair Work Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2009A00028/latest) (secondary)

### Inferences
- **The settled message for the modules.** Excluding trans women from a women's service, or treating trans women staff as "not women", carries federal direct-discrimination risk. That is now final, appellate-level law. The respondent's claim that "women-only is lawful because it is a special measure for women" has been rejected for gender identity claims.
- **Where the modules must be careful.** Tickle concerned a goods and services app. Refuges involve shared accommodation, trauma and safety considerations, and possibly SDA s 32 or state-law arguments. The modules should say Tickle "strongly indicates" the outcome for DFV services, not that it "decides" it.
- **Men's behaviour change (MBC) contracts that say "men".** Under SDA s 5B and Tickle's reasoning, "men" in a funding contract would ordinarily be read to include trans men. Reading it as "cis men only" and refusing trans men or non-binary people would raise gender identity discrimination risk. This is my inference; it has not been tested in court for MBC programs.
- **Federal duty applies now.** Because the Queensland positive duty is not in force (section 2), Module 2's "positive duty" content must be anchored in **SDA s 47C**. Note that s 47C covers *sex*-based conduct. Homophobic or transphobic conduct is captured by s 47C only where it is also sex-based harassment or creates a hostile environment on the ground of sex. Otherwise it falls under WHS duties and the general anti-discrimination prohibitions.

- I could not read the FCAFC 64 judgment text or the AHRC media release directly (403/503). The s 7D "distributive reading" and the constitutional-validity holdings on appeal are therefore secondary-sourced.
- I could not verify AHRC public enforcement actions from 2024–2026, or any current AHRC guidance on single-sex services after Tickle.
- I did not confirm the exact wording of SDA ss 30 and 32 or of FW Act s 351.

## 2. Queensland: Anti-Discrimination Act 2024, Respect at Work and Other Matters Amendment Act 2024, positive duty, GORs, Human Rights Act 2019, QHRC complaints

### Takeaway
The **Anti-Discrimination Act 1991 (Qld)** is the operative Act. The Labor-era reforms are **paused indefinitely**. This includes the Queensland positive duty, the sex-based harassment prohibition, new attributes, intersectional complaints and longer complaint time limits. The Crisafulli government paused them in April–May 2025, and they are now under the **Flanagan review**, whose submissions close **30 October 2026** and whose report is due **31 March 2027**. A Labor Opposition private member's bill (2026) seeks to commence them. **There is no Queensland statutory positive duty in force as at 28 October 2026.**

### Cited Findings
- **The 2025 pause.** On **14 March 2025**, Attorney-General Deb Frecklington announced the pause of "Labor's changes to the Anti-Discrimination Act 1991", passed in September 2024 as the RAW Act and due on 1 July 2025.
  - Commencement would be delayed "until a date set by proclamation".
  - Stated reasons were consultation time and concerns about the new "irrelevant criminal record" attribute.
  - — [Ministerial statement 102168, 14 Mar 2025](https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/102168) (verified)
- **The delaying Act.** It was the **Crime and Corruption (Restoring Reporting Powers) and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2025** (No. 7), assented **19 May 2025**; pt 4 div 1 commenced on assent. The 1991 Act's current reprint is effective **19 May 2025**. — [ADA 1991 legislative history](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-1991-085/lh) (verified)
- **What commenced and what did not (RAW Act 2024, No. 47, assented 19 Sept 2024).**
  - Only limited provisions commenced on **1 December 2024**: ss 3, 28(1), 31, 37, 38, 46–48 and parts of ss 51–52. Section 31 replaced ss 146–152A. The parts of ss 51–52 included the definitions "class member" and "registered employee organisation", which relate to complaint and representation machinery.
  - The rest is "**not yet proclaimed**". That includes new ch 5C, ss 131H–131J (the positive duty), and amendments to ss 15/15A, 120–120E, 124A–124D and ch 7 pt 1A.
  - — [ADA 1991 legislative history](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-1991-085/lh) (verified)
- **What the pause postponed.** According to the Opposition's explanatory note, the postponed changes include:
  - the positive duty;
  - sex-based harassment;
  - DFV and homelessness attributes;
  - vilification protections for disability and age;
  - intersectional complaints;
  - simpler legal tests and longer complaint time limits.
  - — [Strengthening Protections for Queensland Workers Amendment Bill 2026, Explanatory Notes](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/bill.first.exp/bill-2026-016) (verified via PDF text)
- **Opposition bill, 2026.** The **Strengthening Protections for Queensland Workers Amendment Bill 2026** is a **Labor Opposition Private Member's Bill**. Its notes say "the Opposition should not have to bring this Private Members Bill". It would enact the paused reforms into the 1991 Act, including a positive duty broader than SDA s 47C. — [Explanatory Notes](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/bill.first.exp/bill-2026-016) (verified). **Correction:** a Perplexity answer wrongly called this an enacted Act that repealed the ADA 2024. It is a bill, and its passage status is unverified; an opposition bill is unlikely to pass.
- **Status of the Anti-Discrimination Act 2024 itself.** I could not confirm whether the stand-alone ADA 2024 remains on the statute book uncommenced or was repealed. The government's own framing refers to the RAW Act changes to the 1991 Act. — [Statement 102168](https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/102168) (gap)
- **Flanagan review.** Announced **11 June 2026** and led by the Hon Peter Flanagan KC. It has three parts:
  - Part A: QHRC functions and performance;
  - Part B: further review of the **Human Rights Act 2019**;
  - Part C: the **uncommenced RAW Act provisions**.
  - The discussion paper was released **30 Sept 2026**, submissions close **30 Oct 2026**, and the final report is due by **31 March 2027**. The report is not public until tabled.
  - — [DJ review page](https://www.justice.qld.gov.au/initiatives/review-of-the-queensland-human-rights-commission) (verified); [Proctor, Oct 2026](https://www.qlsproctor.com.au/2026/10/qhrc-review-releases-discussion-paper/)
- **Advocacy around the review.** The Australian Christian Lobby is mobilising for stronger religious exemptions and weaker protections in the review. — [National Secular Lobby weekend wrap](https://www.nsl.org.au/columns/weekend-wrap-for-17-october-2026/); [ACL](https://www.acl.org.au/blog/acl-to-defend-christian-freedom-in-queensland-review/) (secondary)
- **ADA 1991 s 7 attributes.** These include **gender identity, sexuality and sex characteristics**. "Sex characteristics" was added, and s 28 (the old "work with children" exemption for lawful sexual activity or gender identity) was **repealed**, by the Births, Deaths and Marriages Registration Act 2023, commenced **29 April 2024**. — [ADA 1991 current text](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-1991-085) and [history](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/inforce/current/act-1991-085/lh) (verified)
- **ADA 1991 s 25, genuine occupational requirements.**
  - s 25(1): "A person may impose genuine occupational requirements for a position". The examples are actors (authenticity), political advisers, considering only women applicants for a job searching women, and teachers at religious schools.
  - s 25(3)–(5): religious-employer provisions with a "not unreasonable" test.
  - s 25(6): excludes age, race and impairment.
  - — [ADA 1991 current text](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-1991-085) (verified; paraphrased)
- **ADA 1991 s 104 (welfare measures) and s 105 (equal opportunity measures).**
  - s 104 includes the example "limiting special accommodation to women who have experienced domestic violence".
  - s 105 lasts only until the purpose is achieved.
  - Gender identity appears in s 111(3), a sport restriction; no specific gender identity exemption appears in the work or services provisions read.
  - — [ADA 1991 current text](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-1991-085) (verified, paraphrased)
- **Human Rights Act 2019 (Qld).**
  - s 9(1)(h): a public entity includes an entity performing "functions of a public nature … for the State or a public entity". Under s 10(1), public funding is a factor.
  - s 10(3) deems certain functions public, including **"a housing service by a funded provider"**, public health and public disability services.
  - s 58(1): it is unlawful to act incompatibly with human rights or to fail to give "proper consideration".
  - s 59(3): no damages on the s 58 ground alone (piggy-back).
  - s 65: a complainant must first complain to the entity and wait **45 business days**.
  - — [HRA 2019](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/current/act-2019-005) (verified; the page showed a 2023-09-20 reprint link, so confirm the current reprint)
- **QHRC complaints under the ADA.** The ordinary time limit is **1 year**; the extension is paused. Complaints go to conciliation, then to **QIRC** for work-related matters or **QCAT** for others. — Perplexity citing [QHRC complaints](https://www.qhrc.qld.gov.au/complaints) (secondary; qhrc.qld.gov.au returned 403)

### Inferences
- **What the modules must say.** "Queensland passed a positive duty in 2024 but it is **not in force**. The government has paused it pending the Flanagan review (report due March 2027). The federal duty in SDA s 47C applies now." Any slide saying "Queensland's positive duty applies from 1 July 2025" would be **wrong**.
- **Content may date in 2027.** This area is the most likely to change during the grant, which ends October 2027. The Flanagan report in March 2027 could lead to proclamation, amendment or repeal, and the HRA itself is under review. The modules need a dated "law as at" statement and a planned refresh point (for example Q2 2027).
- **GORs for "lived experience" roles.** There is no clean Queensland GOR for "must be a cis woman". The s 25 examples are narrow, and gender identity discrimination in work is unlawful. A women's service wanting to exclude trans women staff faces Queensland and federal risk.
  - Conversely, roles targeted *to* queer or trans workers would normally be framed as **s 105 equal opportunity measures** or s 104 welfare measures, or under SDA s 7D, not as a GOR.
  - This is a contested drafting area; get legal review.
- **The s 104 example and trans women.** The example ("special accommodation to women who have experienced domestic violence") supports women-only refuges under Queensland law. Whether it lets a refuge exclude **trans women** is untested. After Tickle's reading of federal s 7D, relying on it for that purpose is risky. Both regimes apply concurrently; a provider must comply with both.
- **HRA obligations.** Funded DFV providers, especially those delivering **housing/accommodation** (s 10(3)) or other contracted government services, are likely functional public entities for those functions. Board decisions on who is served or excluded would then need documented "proper consideration" of human rights, including equality (s 15), privacy and security of person. This is an inference; it is fact-specific.

## 3. Queensland WHS psychosocial hazards, client-perpetrated harm, LGBTIQ+ staff, workers' compensation boundary

### Takeaway
Queensland's WHS psychosocial provisions have been in force since **1 April 2023**. They are supported by the *Managing the risk of psychosocial hazards at work Code of Practice 2022* and, since **1 September 2024**, by specific sexual and sex/gender-based harassment duties, with **prevention plans required from 1 March 2025**. These duties plainly cover harm from clients, including in perpetrator programs. Homophobic, transphobic or misogynistic abuse of staff by clients is a foreseeable psychosocial hazard the PCBU must control. This is legally **settled** and the strongest legal anchor for Module 2.

### Cited Findings
- **Psychosocial provisions.** The WHS Regulation 2011 (Qld), Part 3.2 Div 11 (ss 55A–55D), was inserted by the Work Health and Safety (Psychosocial Risks) Amendment Regulation 2022 and commenced **1 April 2023**.
  - A psychosocial hazard arises from work design or management, the work environment, plant, or "workplace interactions or behaviours".
  - PCBUs must manage the risk under Part 3.1, considering the duration, frequency and severity of exposure.
  - — Perplexity citing [WHS Reg 2011 (Qld)](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/pdf/inforce/current/sl-2011-0240) and [amendment explanatory notes](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/html/published.exp/sl-2022-0141) (secondary; section numbers not personally verified)
- **Code of Practice 2022 hazards.** The listed hazards include aggression or violence; bullying; harassment, including sexual harassment; remote or isolated work; traumatic events; and conflict or poor workplace relationships.
  - The framework covers conduct by clients, customers and other third parties.
  - The Code is drafted in general terms and has no LGBTIQ+-specific section.
  - — Perplexity summary (secondary; worksafe.qld.gov.au returned 403)
- **Sexual harassment amendment.** The Work Health and Safety (Sexual Harassment) Amendment Regulation 2024 commenced **1 September 2024**. It requires PCBUs to manage the risks of sexual harassment and **sex or gender-based harassment**. Written **prevention plans** have been required **from 1 March 2025** and must be reviewed at least every 3 years. — Perplexity citing [WorkSafe comms kit](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0017/134306/sexual-harassment-regs-2024-comms-kit.pdf) and [WorkSafe page](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/safety-and-prevention/mental-health/Psychosocial-hazards/sexual-harassment-and-sex-or-gender-based-harassment) (secondary; page 403 on fetch)
- **Updated guidance.** WorkSafe issued updated guidance on managing workplace psychosocial risks in **August 2025**. — [WorkSafe eSafe Aug 2025](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/news-and-events/newsletters/esafe-newsletters/esafe-editions/esafe/august-2025/updated-guidance-managing-workplace-psychosocial-risks) (not opened)
- **Workers' compensation boundary.** Under the Workers' Compensation and Rehabilitation Act 2003 (Qld) s 32, a psychiatric or psychological injury requires employment to be "**the significant contributing factor**". There is an exclusion for reasonable management action taken in a reasonable way. Compensation is a separate question from the WHS duty. — Perplexity summary citing [WCRA 2003](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/pdf/inforce/2026-06-23/act-2003-027) (secondary). Note that Queensland's test is "the significant contributing factor"; the Perplexity output said "a", and the exact wording should be checked.

### Inferences
- **Perplexity on "sex or gender-based harassment".** Perplexity claimed this term covers gender identity. The modules should not assert that without checking the Regulation definition.
- **Homophobic abuse in MBC programs.** Homophobic abuse of a gay worker by an MBC client may not be "sex or gender-based" but is still squarely a **psychosocial hazard** under the general duty (aggression, harassment, traumatic events). It is safe to teach as settled law.
- **What Module 2 can say with confidence.** "Exposure of queer and trans staff to homophobia, transphobia and misogyny from perpetrator-program clients is a foreseeable psychosocial hazard; PCBUs must identify it, consult workers, and implement controls (co-facilitation, rostering choice, debriefing, escalation, exclusion or consequence rules for clients), and review them." That is a WHS framing. It is less contested than anti-discrimination framing and is enforceable by the regulator.
- **Workers' comp is the fallback, not the strategy.** A claim depends on causation and is not a substitute for prevention.

- I could not verify whether the Regulation's "sex or gender-based harassment" definition explicitly covers gender identity or sexual orientation.
- I found no Queensland enforcement examples on psychosocial hazards in the DFV sector.

## 4. Queensland DFV policy, workforce and political context (LGBTIQ+ relevance)

### Takeaway
The DFV portfolio sits with the **Department of Families, Seniors, Disability Services and Child Safety**, under Minister Amanda Camm (Minister for … Child Safety and the Prevention of Domestic and Family Violence). Coercive control has been a criminal offence since **26 May 2025**. The political climate for trans people in Queensland is **sensitive**: the government has continued the pause on starting puberty blockers and hormones for under-18s until the UK PATHWAYS trial reports (expected 2031), after a court fight in October 2025. Anti-discrimination reform is paused, and religious-lobby advocacy is active in the current review.

### Cited Findings
- **DFV department.** The DFV pages for service providers and grants, including **WorkUP Queensland** (the Queensland Government-funded DFSV workforce development provider, established by The Healing Foundation with ANROWS), sit on families.qld.gov.au, the site of the Department of Families, Seniors, Disability Services and Child Safety. — [families.qld.gov.au: For service providers](https://www.families.qld.gov.au/our-work/domestic-family-sexual-violence/for-service-providers); [WorkUP Queensland page](https://www.families.qld.gov.au/our-work/domestic-family-sexual-violence/for-service-providers/workup-queensland) (search snippets; pages 403 on fetch)
- **Minister.** Perplexity said the minister is Amanda Camm, "Minister for Families, Seniors and Disability Services and Minister for Child Safety and the Prevention of Domestic and Family Violence". It also gave a wrong department acronym (DSDSATSIP), so treat its detail cautiously. — Perplexity (secondary; verify against the current Administrative Arrangements Order)
- **Funding.** The 2024–25 Budget committed $154.4m over 4 years to DFSV ([statement 100446](https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/100446)). The Commonwealth committed $67.2m over 4 years to continue the "500 Workers Initiative" to 2030 ([Redland City Bulletin](https://redlandbaysidenews.com.au/gender-violence-worker-funding/)) (secondary).
- **Coercive control.** The offence is **Criminal Code s 334C**, commenced **26 May 2025**, with a maximum penalty of 14 years. It came from the Women's Safety and Justice Taskforce (Hear Her Voice) reforms. Qld Government coercive control materials recognise threats to disclose a person's gender, sexuality or HIV status. — [qld.gov.au coercive control laws](https://www.qld.gov.au/initiative/need-to-know/coercive-control/coercive-control-laws); [statement 102613](https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/102613) (via Perplexity; not opened)
- **Practice standards.** The Queensland Practice Principles, Standards and Guidance for DFV service providers and the Professional Practice Standards for working with men who perpetrate DFV exist. Perplexity said they reference LGBTIQ+ clients generally but have limited trans-specific detail. — Perplexity (unverified; treat as a gap)
- **Puberty blocker pause, January 2025.** On **28 January 2025** a Queensland Health directive paused **new** Stage 1 and 2 treatment for under-18s, and the Vine review was commissioned. — [Parliament QoN 831-2025](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/tableoffice/questionsanswers/2025/831-2025.pdf); [Queensland Health review page](https://www.health.qld.gov.au/research-reports/reports/review-investigation/hormone-therapies-review)
- **October 2025 court challenge.** On 28 Oct 2025 the Supreme Court (Callaghan J) set the directive aside for procedural failure. The Health Minister reinstated the ban by his own directive the same day. — [ABC, 28 Oct 2025](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-10-28/qld-puberty-blockers-judgement/105942094); [ABC, 29 Oct 2025](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2025-10-29/qld-health-minister-puberty-blockers-youth-supreme-court/105944876)
- **Extension to 2031.** On **19 December 2025**, Health Minister Tim Nicholls announced the hold on starting Stage 1 and 2 treatment in public hospitals will continue until the UK PATHWAYS trial results, expected **2031**; existing patients continue. — [statement 104227](https://statements.qld.gov.au/statements/104227) (verified)
- **Birth certificates.** The BDM Registration Act 2023 sex-descriptor framework (in force from 24 June 2024) still appears in force in 2026 reprints, and no repeal was found. — Perplexity citing [legislation.qld.gov.au](https://www.legislation.qld.gov.au/view/whole/html/inforce/2026-07-05/act-2023-017) (secondary)

### Inferences
- **Name the funder correctly.** The QTWAV grant is most likely administered through the Department of Families, Seniors, Disability Services and Child Safety's DFSV area. This should be confirmed against QTWAV's grant agreement.
- **Political sensitivity is real.** The government has twice acted against expert or court-driven positions on trans young people's care, and has paused anti-discrimination reform. Module 1's content on trans women in women's services and on "leading on values" will therefore be read in a charged environment. Gender-critical groups and religious lobbies are active.
  - Content should be precise, sourced and dated, so that it cannot be attacked as overstating the law. For example: "Tickle is final federal law" is safe. "Refuges must admit all trans women" is overstated.
- **Funder alignment.** The modules are funded by a Queensland Government grant. A careful review of the language describing government policy, especially health policy, is prudent to avoid funder-relations risk. This is a reputational issue, not a legal one.

## 5. Settled vs contested points, and what external review is prudent (including cost and pro bono availability)

### Inferences

**Settled points: state confidently, with citations**
- Gender identity, sexuality and sex characteristics protections exist under the ADA 1991 (Qld) and the SDA.
- Tickle is final: excluding a trans woman because she is trans can be direct discrimination, and a "women" special measure does not answer that.
- SDA s 47C is the operative positive duty.
- WHS psychosocial duties cover client-perpetrated harm.
- HRA ss 58–59 bind public entities, with no stand-alone damages.

**Contested or changing points: present as "current position plus uncertainty"**
- The Queensland positive duty and the sex-based harassment and attribute reforms are paused pending the Flanagan review (report due March 2027).
- Whether, and on what basis, women-only refuges or programs may exclude trans women: SDA s 32, ADA s 104 and safety arguments have not been tested in a DFV case.
- Lawful framing of targeted recruitment of queer and trans workers: special measures and s 105 versus GOR.
- Whether a particular NGO is an HRA functional public entity.
- How funding-contract terms ("men", "women") interact with discrimination law. Contract interpretation is fact-specific.
- Whether constitutions with "women" in their objects need amendment. This involves charity and ACNC law plus discrimination law and needs legal input.

**Recommended review model**
1. **Legal accuracy review (recommended, required before launch).** Approach the **LGBTI Legal Service** first. It has the most aligned expertise, existing links with the Queensland LGBTI sector, and a pro bono network. In parallel, have QTWAV's auspice (Queensland Council for LGBTI Health, a not-for-profit) apply to **LawRight Pro Bono Connect**. Capyble itself, as a business, is generally ineligible.
   - Scope: a bounded review of the scripts against Queensland and federal law, with tracked changes and a short issues memo. Explicitly an educational-accuracy review, not advice to learners.
   - Budget a paid fallback of roughly A$3,000–7,500 + GST (estimate, unsourced) in case pro bono is unavailable within the timeline.
2. **Subject-matter and practice review.** A Respect@Work/WHS specialist should review Module 2 (positive duty, psychosocial controls). A DFV practice lead should review MBC and refuge scenarios. Basic Rights Queensland and Working Women Queensland are employment and discrimination CLCs focused on individual clients; they are better as consultation or referral partners than as formal content reviewers. Their capacity for third-party review is unverified.
3. **Currency mechanism.** Include a "law stated as at [date]" banner and disclaimers. Plan a **post-March 2027 refresh check** (Flanagan report; any proclamation of the RAW provisions; any SDA amendment push after Tickle). Factor this into the proposal timeline and budget before the grant ends in October 2027.

**Why not a law-firm-only review.** A large firm's pro bono review may be more conservative and slower. A CLC or LGBTI-specialist reviewer better understands the lived-experience framing. The ideal is an LGBTI Legal Service review with a pro bono firm partner.
